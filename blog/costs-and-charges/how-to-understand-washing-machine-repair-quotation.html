How to understand a washing machine repair quotation

A useful washing machine repair quotation tells you what is wrong, what will be done and the complete amount you are being asked to approve. A single number is difficult to compare if you cannot tell whether it includes a new part, a repair to the existing component, fitting, inspection or tax.

Use this FixMyWasher guide to review a quote line by line. It includes illustrative calculations, a comparison checklist and questions for major repairs. The examples explain the arithmetic and scope; they are not records of actual customer jobs, fixed prices or promises of automatic discounts.

You should be able to explain the proposed job back in ordinary language. If a quote only says “machine repair”, ask for enough detail to know what result is intended and which parts of the appliance are outside the scope.

Start with the finding, not the component price

The proposed repair should follow a diagnosis. “Not spinning” describes a symptom; it does not identify a motor failure by itself. Ask what was observed and why the recommended action addresses the problem. You do not need to carry out tests yourself to request that explanation.

A quotation may be provisional if the model, internal damage or part availability has not yet been confirmed. That is acceptable when the uncertainty is explicit. Ask which amount is firm, which is an estimate and what further assessment is needed before the final price can be agreed.

For a larger job, check whether any other known fault remains. A quote that restores drainage but leaves a separately confirmed mechanical problem unresolved should say so. This helps you judge the value of the whole repair decision rather than only the cheapest first step.

Read the part description carefully

A new component, a repaired original component and a refurbished or customer-supplied item are different propositions. The quote should use the correct description. A board repaired and refitted is not the same as a newly supplied control board, even if both options are intended to resolve the same complaint.

Ask for the exact component or assembly and its compatibility with the machine. Where available, a part reference or clear description helps distinguish a pump motor from a complete pump assembly, or a bearing kit from a complete tub. Do not compare those prices as if the supplied items were identical.

If a quote uses “genuine”, ask which manufacturer or source that description refers to and whether it is supported by the item actually supplied. Compatibility, condition and provenance are separate questions. This site does not claim that FixMyWasher is a manufacturer-authorised service centre.

Check what the labour line includes

A parts-only price will need a labour discussion. A supplied-and-fitted price already includes the stated fitting work. Ask whether normal dismantling, refitting, relevant connections and completion checks are in that amount. The same operation should not appear twice under slightly different labels without explanation.

For combined repairs, ask how shared access work is priced. Replacing two accessible parts in one visit and completing two independent standalone jobs may involve different labour. There is no universal combined-job discount here, but the scope should make the charge understandable.

Some tasks require special access, transport or off-site work. If those are proposed, ask why, what arrangements apply and whether the amount is included. A quote should not leave you to discover that the advertised fitting price excludes the access required for your actual installation.

Do not add alternative repairs or overlapping assemblies

A quote may present options: repair the existing PCB, or fit a new PCB. Those are alternatives unless the technician explains a different combined scope. Adding both amounts together can exaggerate the expected bill and obscure the choice you are being asked to make.

The same issue arises with mechanical assemblies. A bearing-only repair and replacement of a complete tub are not automatically cumulative charges. Ask whether the larger assembly already contains the smaller components and what additional items are genuinely required.

Optional cleaning, descaling or accessories should be clearly separated from the fault correction. You can ask which work is essential to the proposed repair and which is a separate recommendation. Approval should identify the option and extras you actually choose.

How to handle “subject to inspection” and changing prices

A preliminary estimate can help you decide whether to book, but it is not the final diagnosis. Ask what could move the quote outside the initial range: a different model-specific part, additional confirmed damage or access requirements are examples that need explanation.

If further dismantling is necessary, agree the next stage before it begins. Ask what that stage costs, what happens if you stop afterward and whether reassembly is included. This prevents a small inspection enquiry from becoming an open-ended repair approval.

When the scope changes, request the revised component list, reason and total. Keep the old and new versions or messages together. “There is more damage” needs enough detail to understand the additional work, without requiring you to become an appliance engineer.

Clarify timing, orders and payment arrangements

If parts must be ordered, ask about compatibility confirmation, expected availability and how the fitting visit will be arranged. An availability estimate should not be treated as a guaranteed completion date unless it is actually confirmed. Mention if timing is important before authorising the order.

Ask whether a deposit is required, what it covers and which order or cancellation terms apply. No universal deposit percentage or refund policy is invented in this guide. The arrangements should be clear for the actual part and job.

At completion, the charged amount should be understandable against the approved quote and changes. Keep the receipt or job record. If a final invoice contains an unfamiliar line, ask what work or supplied item it represents before treating it as part of the original agreement.

Connect payment and paperwork to the completed scope

Ask the technician to explain the relevant checks after the repair. A drainage job should be assessed for the affected water movement and leakage where practical; a door repair needs the relevant closure and operating checks. Verification should follow the repaired function and the model.

A successful limited test does not establish that every other component is new or that an intermittent fault can never recur. If the original symptom could not be reproduced or a full relevant check could not be completed, have that limitation explained.

The job record should identify the work, the supplied part where applicable and any remaining recommendation. Do not accept a vague whole-machine warranty statement in place of the actual warranty terms for the component and service you bought.

Apply FixMyWasher’s visiting and inspection terms correctly

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Share your Bangalore locality or PIN code and confirm all applicable charges together before booking. ₹200 is not an advertised complete repair or parts-replacement price.

Ask how the visiting and inspection charges are treated if a repair proceeds. No automatic waiver, deduction or additional charge should be assumed. Your approved quote should make clear whether these amounts are included, credited or payable separately, and state any applicable tax and the final payable total.

When reviewing a quote, look for the booking-charge treatment in words rather than inferring it from subtraction. If it is absent, ask for clarification. The worked ₹300 inspection example above is solely an arithmetic illustration and does not set a location-based inspection tariff.

Read the warranty beside the part description

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

If the quote offers both an existing-board repair and a new-board replacement, ask for the terms beside each option. A one-year new-parts warranty should not be copied across to an existing-component repair without a separately confirmed agreement.

A short approval message you can adapt

Before approving, you can write: “Please confirm the identified fault, the exact repair or new part, included fitting and checks, treatment of visit and inspection charges, any tax and the full payable total. Please also confirm the warranty on the supplied part and ask me before any additional work.”

Add your chosen option if the quote lists alternatives. If you are postponing or declining, ask for the no-repair total and the safe condition in which the machine will be left. Clear approval is about a defined scope and amount, not only saying yes to a general service enquiry.

Frequently asked questions

What should a washing machine repair quotation include?

It should identify the machine, diagnosed fault, proposed work and part, labour and checks, other charges, full payable total, approval conditions and applicable warranty terms.

How do I know whether labour is already included?

Look for wording such as supplied and fitted or parts plus fitting. Ask which fitting and testing operations are covered before adding a separate labour line.

Is an estimate the same as a final quote?

A preliminary estimate may depend on model, inspection and part availability. Ask which amounts are confirmed and what must be established before the final scope and total are agreed.

Should I compare a repaired board with a new-board price?

You can compare them as different repair options, but not as identical products. Check suitability, scope, condition and the separate warranty terms for each option.

Is inspection always deducted from the repair bill?

No automatic deduction is stated. A credit applies only when explicitly agreed. The worked credit in this guide is an illustration, not a standard policy.

Should the quote show whether tax is included?

Ask for clear tax treatment and the complete payable amount. Do not assume a tax rate or compare an inclusive total with a subtotal that excludes applicable tax.

What if the technician finds additional damage?

Request the new finding, revised work and complete total before approving additional work. Keep the agreed change with the original quotation.

Can I choose only one option from the quotation?

Yes, if the options are alternatives. Clarify which correction is necessary, which extras are optional and whether any other confirmed fault would remain.

What warranty applies to a new FixMyWasher part?

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. It applies to the identified new part, while labour, revisits, workmanship and existing-component repair terms need separate confirmation.

What should I keep after the repair?

Keep the quote, approved changes, receipt or job record, supplied-part description and warranty details. These help explain the completed work if a later enquiry is needed.
