How to Level a Washing Machine: Stability and Setup Checks

Start with a suitable floor, confirm the exact model’s levelling method, and check both level and stable support at the final position. If the washer moves violently, stop using it and arrange assessment. Turning a foot repeatedly is not a diagnosis for every spin problem.

This guide is for owners preparing a levelling check or assessing what an installer has done. It explains the evidence to collect and the decisions to make. Adjustment that requires lifting, tipping, reaching beneath the machine or moving stacked equipment belongs with suitably equipped help.

Six steps to a clear setup decision

  1. Identify the machine and arrangement

    Use the full model and installation section. State whether the washer is floor-mounted, on an approved pedestal or stacked. A different arrangement can change access and the applicable method.

  2. Establish a safe starting condition

    Switch off and follow the manual’s isolation instructions. Do not work around wet electrical equipment. If access requires lifting or tilting, stop the owner check and arrange help.

  3. Review the floor and final position

    Describe a sloping, damaged, flexible or slippery surface and any contact with surrounding cabinets. Levelling feet cannot be assumed to correct every unsuitable support condition.

  4. Have the applicable levelling method followed

    Use the model’s reference surfaces, adjustment limits and securing method. Confirm support at all required contact points; do not import a clockwise rule from another design.

  5. Recheck connections and permitted operation

    After positioning, verify that hoses and the cable have not been trapped or strained. Use the appropriate commissioning procedure only after the physical setup is complete.

  6. Record the remaining symptom

    If movement continues, record when it occurs, the load and what was checked. Request a targeted assessment instead of buying pads or internal parts by guesswork.

Level and stable are related but different checks

Level describes the orientation of the machine using the reference and method the manufacturer specifies. Stable support describes whether the machine rests properly on its intended contact points. An appliance can appear horizontal yet have a poorly supported foot; a cabinet that does not rock can still be tilted.

Ask the installer to explain both observations. A decorative top edge, curved lid or uneven floor tile is not automatically the correct measuring reference. The exact instructions decide where and how a level reading should be taken.

Do not use a phone placed on an arbitrary surface as the final acceptance test. It may help you describe an obvious tilt, but the accuracy and reference surface still matter. A clear method is more valuable than a confident reading from an unsuitable position.

The practical outcome is a correctly supported installation that meets the model requirements, with the relevant operating check recorded. It is not a promise that the machine will be completely motionless or silent on every cycle.

Check whether the floor is suitable before adjusting feet

Look at the actual supporting area, not only the room as a whole. A loose tile, local dip, slope or flexible platform can affect the position where the washer stands. Explain those conditions to the installer before asking them to compensate through adjustment.

A bathroom or utility floor may slope toward a drain. That does not automatically mean a washer can be installed anywhere on it. The available adjustment and intended support must be assessed against the model and the site.

Do not place folded cardboard, loose wood, bricks or an improvised wedge under a foot to make the cabinet seem steady. These materials can change under load or moisture and can conceal a support problem instead of resolving it.

If the floor needs building work, separate that task from appliance adjustment. Agree what must be corrected and who is responsible before booking another commissioning attempt. The installer should not be expected to certify a structural alteration outside the agreed work.

Avoid recreating violent movement for a video. Existing safe observations are enough to start the conversation. If a recording is already available, share it with the programme and installation history rather than treating the sound alone as a component diagnosis.

What to ask about the actual adjustment

Ask which feet are adjustable on your model, how their permitted range is established and how the final setting is secured. Designs differ, so this guide does not prescribe one direction of rotation, tool size or maximum extension for every machine.

If a foot is damaged, seized or cannot provide the required support within its allowed range, more force is not the answer. Have the condition assessed and obtain a compatible replacement or a site correction as appropriate.

Do not loosen unrelated fasteners underneath the appliance. A bolt that happens to be visible is not necessarily a levelling adjustment. Keep hands and tools out of unsupported spaces under the cabinet.

After adjustment, ask whether the prescribed securing step was completed. Samsung’s installation guidance, for the models it covers, distinguishes adjustment from securing the setting and checking floor contact. That principle is useful; the exact procedure still comes from your machine’s instructions.

Separate levelling from transport preparation

A newly delivered or moved front loader may need its documented transport restraints addressed before operation. Levelling does not make retained transport restraints safe, and restraint removal does not complete levelling.

Use the transit-bolt guide when identification or removal status is uncertain. Do not remove every rear fastener because the washer shakes. Record who prepared the appliance and whether the model-specific transport items were retained for a later move.

For a moved washer that previously worked, ask about the entire sequence: preparation, handling, destination floor and reconnection. This can identify an installation question without prematurely blaming either the mover or the machine.

If the washer was operated before preparation was confirmed, stop further testing and explain what happened. A subsequent quiet run alone does not establish whether all concerns have been resolved.

Pads, stands and pedestals need a defined purpose

An accessory should solve an identified requirement and suit the actual appliance. Do not buy anti-vibration pads simply because a seller promises that every washer becomes silent with them. Ask whether the manufacturer permits the proposed arrangement and what problem it addresses.

A pedestal changes height and access. A movable stand changes the support arrangement. A mat changes the contact surface. These are not interchangeable products, and none automatically corrects transport preparation, loading or an internal fault.

If an accessory was recently added, mention it when describing the onset of movement. Keep its identity and installation instructions available. The technician needs to understand the current configuration rather than assess the machine as if it sits directly on the floor.

For stacked equipment, use the approved combination and kit and arrange appropriate handling. Do not attempt to reduce vibration by loosening a stacking attachment or shifting the upper appliance yourself.

A load-related problem needs a load-related record

Record whether the concern occurs with every permitted load or mainly with one type. A single large absorbent item, an unsuitable programme or an exceeded programme limit deserves a different review from a cabinet that rocks before any laundry is added.

Use the garment labels and the actual programme load limit. The headline capacity is not the allowable load for every specialist cycle. Do not deliberately overload the washer to test whether a levelling adjustment worked.

If the manual provides an owner response to an unbalanced load, follow that procedure. Never reach into a moving drum or defeat the lock. A pause and safe access sequence may differ between machines.

If the problem persists with suitable loading and confirmed setup, ask for diagnosis. Repeated foot adjustment without new evidence can delay identifying the real cause.

Write the original observation before describing the change. For example, record that the off-state cabinet rocked, that the installer adjusted and secured the relevant support, and that the agreed check was then completed. Avoid writing “suspension repaired” if no suspension work took place.

Keep the conditions of the check clear. An empty setup procedure and a normal laundry cycle answer different questions. If operation could not be checked, record that limitation and the next step instead of leaving an unconditional completion statement.

Choose an adjustment visit or diagnosis deliberately

If the only known issue is setup after a move, ask for a reinstallation or levelling review with the scope stated. If a machine that previously operated normally develops a new symptom without being moved, provide that history so the provider can decide what assessment is appropriate.

Ask what the appointment includes: safe access, adjustment, inspection of supplied feet, final positioning and relevant verification. Replacement parts, special handling and floor corrections need explicit agreement.

Use the handover checklist to retain the completed work and any pending item. If a second provider must correct the site, record the sequence so the washer is not repeatedly tested at an unsuitable location.

Understand the installation and supplied-part terms

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Installation labour, adjustments and a setup check without a supplied new part do not create a one-year whole-machine warranty. Confirm workmanship and revisit terms separately. Check any installation included with a new purchase before arranging independent work; FixMyWasher does not claim manufacturer-authorised status.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know whether my washer needs levelling?

An off-state support concern or an installation change justifies a setup review. Spin movement alone does not identify its cause.

Is a centred bubble enough?

No. The correct reference surface and stable support also matter.

Can I use cardboard under a foot?

Do not improvise a support. Use the permitted arrangement and correct the underlying site condition.

Should every foot turn the same way?

Do not assume a universal adjustment direction or design. Follow the exact model instructions.

Can I tip the machine alone to reach a foot?

Do not undertake unsupported lifting or tipping. Arrange suitable assistance.

Do vibration pads repair the problem?

They are not a universal remedy. Establish the cause and compatibility before buying accessories.

Does calibration replace levelling?

No. An applicable calibration procedure does not correct an unsuitable physical installation.

Why does it move only with one load?

The programme and load need review as well as the setup. Record those details without guessing an internal fault.

Should I hold it still while it spins?

No. Stop use if movement is violent and obtain assessment.

Is levelling covered by a one-year whole-machine warranty?

No such promise applies. Confirm workmanship and revisit terms; supplied new parts have separate coverage.
