Washing machine transit bolts: confirm the transport fittings before use

On a washer that uses transit bolts, the specified transport restraints must be removed as instructed before normal operation and dealt with correctly before a future move. Identify them using the exact installation manual. Do not remove every rear fastener, copy another model’s bolt count or run a spin to find out whether restraints are still fitted.

This guide explains the difference between transport preparation and washing configuration, what to ask the installer, and how to handle missing or uncertain parts. It is not a universal bolt-removal procedure: location, number, sleeves, caps and access vary.

Six stages for resolving a restraint question

  1. Identify the full model and situation

    State whether the machine is new, already used or about to move. Find the exact installation or transport section. Include the production reference if the manufacturer needs it to distinguish versions.

  2. Match the documented transport assembly

    Use the actual diagram to distinguish transport restraints from cabinet fasteners and other parts. If the diagram or the physical arrangement is unclear, ask for manufacturer or technician assistance before any removal.

  3. Arrange the correct preparation for the next task

    For normal use, the installer should complete the specified removal and finishing steps. For transport, arrange the specified restraint or packing procedure. These are different configurations, so be explicit about which is required.

  4. Check the completion explanation

    Ask which components were handled, whether the full assembly was accounted for and how any specified closures were fitted. Do not accept “some screws removed” as enough information to resolve a model-specific question.

  5. Retain and label the relevant items

    Keep transport components that the manufacturer says to retain, together with the model reference and instructions. Record any part that was missing or replaced. Do not mix similar-looking sets from different appliances.

  6. Proceed only after the whole setup is ready

    Restraint preparation is one part of installation or moving. Confirm the remaining site, connection and commissioning requirements before normal use, or the handling requirements before transport. If the machine was operated prematurely, obtain the appropriate assessment first.

Why a transport fitting can be wrong for normal operation

Transport restraints serve a different purpose from the machine’s normal support system during washing and spinning. Samsung India describes shipping bolts that secure the tub during transportation on its covered front loaders and warns against operating with that restraint left in place.

That distinction explains why the installer cannot simply leave transport components fitted as an extra precaution. It also explains why a washer prepared for use may require a different preparation before being transported again.

The general principle does not identify your exact assembly. A diagram for a different brand or model may show different locations or associated parts. The correct manual remains the practical reference for the work.

Avoid a diagnosis based only on the presence of a rear bolt. There are fasteners on a washing machine that are not transport restraints. Removing the wrong one can create a new problem without answering the original question.

What a complete removal task should account for

The task should begin from the correct instructions and safe access. If reaching the back requires moving a heavy, stacked or enclosed appliance, that access is part of the job. Pulling the washer forward by hoses to save time is not an appropriate shortcut.

The responsible person should account for the documented assembly and the finishing steps. If a sleeve, spacer or other part is described in the manual, do not assume the task is complete simply because the visible metal fastener is out.

If a fastener is damaged, stuck or inconsistent with the diagram, stop forcing it and obtain the correct assistance. Drilling, cutting or removing nearby panels to chase a suspected piece goes beyond this owner guide.

After preparation, the installer still needs to complete the rest of the setup. Removing the transport restraints does not establish correct support, water connections or drainage. Use the handover checklist for that broader completion conversation.

What if the machine has already run with bolts suspected?

Stop use and report what happened. Include whether the machine was new or moved, which cycle was attempted, any severe vibration and the available installation history. Do not repeat a spin to produce clearer evidence.

Do not assume either that the machine is certainly damaged or that removing the restraints will prove it is unharmed. The condition needs to be assessed in context, especially after violent movement, leakage or unusual noise.

For a new appliance, keep the purchase and installer details and use the appropriate seller or manufacturer route for the concern. This page does not determine responsibility or coverage from a remote description.

If an independent assessment is requested, ask what it can establish and what remains uncertain. A successful brief test should not be described as a guarantee that every internal component escaped damage. Keep the recorded findings with the job documents.

Missing bolts before a move: solve the specification first

Give the full model to the manufacturer or an appropriate parts provider and ask for the specified transport set. Clarify whether the set includes associated sleeves or other pieces. A photograph and a guessed bolt diameter are not enough to establish compatibility.

Do not use hardware-store bolts or a set from a different washer because the threads appear to enter. Length and assembly details can matter. The aim is the specified restraint arrangement, not merely filling visible holes.

If the correct item cannot be obtained before the move, seek the manufacturer’s appropriate guidance and discuss the schedule with the mover. Do not invent an exemption for a short trip or a careful driver.

Keep the parts enquiry separate from a transport-service promise. An appliance technician may be able to advise on preparation or installation without supplying packing staff, lifting equipment or a vehicle. Confirm the actual available scope.

Store useful transport information with the parts

Keep retained components in a labelled bag or container associated with the full model. Include the relevant manual page or a note of where the instructions are stored. This is especially useful if several similar appliances are in the same household or building.

Record which items were supplied by the manufacturer and which were later replaced. A future installer should not have to guess whether an unlabeled bag belongs to this washer or a previous one.

Do not leave loose hardware inside the drum during normal use. Keep it in a safe storage location away from children and where it can be found before a move. The manual determines which pieces must be retained, reused or replaced.

When handing the appliance to another owner, transfer the model information and relevant retained parts with an honest condition description. Do not describe missing transport components as unnecessary simply because you no longer need them.

An owner sees several different rear screws and has no manual

Identify the model and correct diagram before anything is loosened. The question is which assembly the instructions identify, not how many screws a video removed.

A newly installed machine moved violently during its first spin

Stop use and request a review of preparation, installation and condition. Do not restart it simply to demonstrate the motion.

A household finds a bag of bolts from its previous washer

Do not assume the set suits the current machine. Verify the correct transport parts before the move.

Understand the installation and supplied-part terms

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Installation labour, adjustments and a setup check without a supplied new part do not create a one-year whole-machine warranty. Confirm workmanship and revisit terms separately. Check any installation included with a new purchase before arranging independent work; FixMyWasher does not claim manufacturer-authorised status.

Frequently asked questions

Are transit bolts and shipping bolts the same term?

They commonly refer to transport restraints on applicable washers. Confirm the actual assembly and terminology in your model manual.

How many bolts should I remove?

Use the exact model diagram. There is no universal number, and unrelated rear fasteners must not be removed by guesswork.

Do top loaders always have the same bolts?

No. Different designs use different transport preparations; follow the actual instructions.

Can I leave transit bolts in for extra stability?

Not where the manual requires their removal for operation. Transport restraint and normal washing configuration serve different purposes.

What if I already ran a wash?

Stop use if restraints are suspected and arrange review. Do not assume either certain damage or certain safety from a remote description.

Can I buy any bolt that fits the hole?

No. Obtain the specified compatible set and associated parts using the full model reference.

Can I move a washer a short distance without required preparation?

Do not invent a distance exemption. Follow the model’s transport guidance and obtain appropriate assistance.

Should I throw away the removed parts?

Retain items as directed by the manufacturer and label them with the model for future use.

Does removing bolts solve every vibration complaint?

No. Support, installation, load and component condition are separate questions that may need review.

Does this page replace the installation manual?

No. It helps you identify the correct task and ask the right questions; the model-specific instructions determine the actual procedure.
