Ask what the word warranty refers to in your quotation

A broad phrase such as service warranty can refer to different things in different offers. Ask whether it concerns a supplied component, work on an existing assembly, fitting labour or another defined task. The answer should match the actual repair method.

For FixMyWasher, the confirmed term is one year on new parts supplied under written terms. Do not stretch that statement into a new warranty on every part of the washer, the property installation or all future faults.

If another type of work is proposed, request its own clear terms before approval. This page does not invent a separate workmanship period, free-revisit promise or coverage on items the provider did not supply. A precise explanation gives the household a better basis for comparing offers.

Clarify coverage before accepting the repair method

Ask for the proposed component or task, its condition and the complete installed total. Then ask which written terms apply. Coverage is easier to understand when the underlying work is already defined.

If two options are offered, keep their methods and terms separate. Repairing an existing assembly and supplying a new component can have different costs, uncertainties and follow-up arrangements. Do not assume the new-part term automatically attaches to both.

Where coverage wording is unclear, ask for clarification before approving the task or a part order. A decision based on a broad verbal promise can become difficult later if the invoice identifies a different method or leaves the covered item unspecified.

Make the covered component identifiable

The invoice or accompanying record should connect the new part to the correct washer and job. Keep the full model, component description, work date and written terms available. If several machines were serviced, the component should be linked to the right unit.

Ask whether the item is new manufacturer-original or a new compatible alternative and what establishes suitability for the model. Those are different descriptions, and the record should reflect the actual supplied item rather than using the word genuine without explanation.

If the proposed item changes during sourcing, clarify the revised description and terms before it is fitted. A warranty record should not refer vaguely to the part originally discussed when a different arrangement was ultimately approved.

Ask how the one-year period and follow-up are recorded

Confirm the relevant start information and how it appears in the job documentation. Do not guess from a message date, advance payment or initial enquiry if the actual work occurred later. The provider’s written record should make the arrangement understandable.

Ask which contact to use for a concern and what information will help identify the job. Keep the invoice and component details accessible. If another person manages the appliance, ensure they know where the record is kept.

This page does not supply unconfirmed exclusions, transfer rules or a replacement-cycle policy. If those details matter to your decision, ask the provider to explain the actual written terms. Clear questions are preferable to treating silence as a promise in either direction.

A repeated symptom still needs an accurate current report

If the washer shows a similar problem after repair, describe the current behaviour, programme stage and complete code. Say when it began and whether the machine worked normally after the earlier job. Keep the original complaint and work record available.

The same visible symptom does not automatically establish the same cause. It may or may not involve the supplied component. The provider needs the appropriate assessment before explaining how the written terms apply to the current finding.

Do not continue using a machine with shock, burning, smoke, repeated tripping or water near electrical equipment while debating coverage. Stop use and arrange appropriate help for the hazard. The coverage conversation should follow an accurate and safe report of the present condition.

Prepare a focused follow-up message

A useful message identifies the earlier job, full model, supplied component if known and current concern. For example: “The washer repaired on the recorded job date is stopping before spin again. The invoice identifies the supplied part. Please advise the assessment and applicable warranty process.” This is an illustrative format, not a customer case.

Include existing safe evidence where relevant and say whether any other work or installation change occurred afterward. Do not hide a change because you assume it will affect coverage; accurate history helps establish the current condition.

Ask what the next step is and which charges, if any, require confirmation. A message reporting a symptom does not by itself establish that every future visit or unrelated task is covered. Keep the provider’s explanation connected to the actual written terms.

Clarify assessment, travel and labour on a return visit

Ask how a reported concern will be assessed and which revisit or labour terms apply. The new-part warranty statement alone does not answer every question about travel, diagnosis or work involving another component.

Do not assume either that every return visit is free or that every return must be charged. Request the applicable arrangement for the actual report. If a separate fault is identified, ask for the explanation and quotation before approving additional work.

Keep the outcome factual: which part or function was assessed, what was found and how the written terms were applied. That record is more useful than a broad statement that warranty was accepted or rejected without explaining the component and finding involved.

When you bought the component from someone else

An externally purchased item has its own supplier and agreed coverage. If a provider is asked to fit it, confirm willingness, compatibility checks and the fitting scope before ordering. Do not assume a technician must install a part simply because you have already paid for it.

Ask who handles an unsuitable item, a supply defect, fitting concerns and the unresolved original fault. Those responsibilities can be different in a split purchase-and-fitting arrangement. Keep the seller’s record and the fitter’s record distinct.

FixMyWasher’s confirmed one-year term is for new parts supplied by FixMyWasher under written terms. This page does not extend that promise to every external purchase. Any accepted fitting or other work needs its own clear agreement.

Keep manufacturer coverage separate from independent repair terms

If manufacturer warranty, component coverage or another service plan may still apply, verify the relevant route before authorising independent work. Use the actual model and documents rather than a remembered advertisement.

FixMyWasher is an independent provider. Its new-part warranty is not a manufacturer warranty claim, and the presence of a brand name on a component does not establish that the entire job is manufacturer-authorised.

If both an earlier provider and a new service arrangement are involved, keep the records and responsibilities clear. This guide does not decide eligibility under another organisation’s terms. Ask the relevant provider to explain its own coverage and process.

Report moves, new work and changed installation honestly

If the washer has moved, another provider has worked on it or the installation changed after the repair, include that history in a follow-up enquiry. These facts may help assess the present concern; they should not be replaced by assumptions about automatic acceptance or rejection of coverage.

Do not dismantle the appliance yourself to prove that the supplied part is faulty. Keep available records and let the appropriate professional establish the finding under safe conditions. If a component is removed for agreed assessment, document identity, custody and return arrangements.

Where the user or property contact changes, keep the invoice and written terms with the machine record. Ask the provider about any applicable transfer or contact arrangement rather than assuming a change in resident creates or removes a particular term.

Keep a small, complete record instead of a broad promise

Retain the final approved scope, invoice, supplied component description and written terms. Add the relevant assessment and completion outcome so the record explains both what was done and what was checked.

If a follow-up occurs, keep its finding and any new agreement with the same job history. Do not assume the coverage period restarts, expands or changes unless the provider’s actual written terms say so. Ask for any change to be clarified.

A useful warranty arrangement is one the customer can understand before approval and refer to later. It should identify the actual covered item and process without claiming that every future washing-machine problem has been prepaid or prevented.

Visit charges and the price of the actual work

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Confirm the applicable booking and inspection amounts, and how they are treated if you approve work, before scheduling. The ₹200 visiting amount is not the price of a complete repair or a promise of a free inspection.

The repair total depends on the brand, full model, diagnosed fault, required part, labour and access. Ask for the complete payable amount before approval, including any separately agreed transport, additional visit or tax. A symptom described on the phone can guide the enquiry but may not support a fixed final price.

Keep the diagnosis and quotation together. If the scope changes after assessment, ask for the revised explanation and total before additional work begins. Any property plumbing or electrical work must be identified and priced separately where required.

New parts, compatibility and your one-year warranty

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. The invoice should identify the covered component and written terms. This is not automatically a warranty on the entire washing machine, every future fault or the property installation.

Ask whether a proposed component is manufacturer-original, a compatible alternative or repair of the existing assembly. These are different offers. Confirm the full model match, condition, sourcing and installed price before approving it. A “universal” description alone does not establish suitability.

Labour, workmanship, repairs to existing components and revisit terms need separate confirmation. Keep the invoice and contact the provider with the original job details if a concern returns. Where manufacturer warranty or a service plan may still apply, check that route before authorising independent work.

Prepare access without opening or moving the machine

Keep the model from your invoice or an existing safe label photograph, the complete error message and any previous repair record ready. Clear ordinary access only where it is safe. Tell us about stairs, a stacked installation, a tight enclosure or a locked utility area before the appointment.

Do not dismantle panels, force a locked door, tilt a heavy washer or recreate a dangerous symptom for a video. If there is a shock, smoke, burning, severe movement or water near electrics, stop use and keep away; arrange the appropriate urgent help before a routine appliance visit. Use an isolation control only if it is safely accessible without approaching the hazard.

Safe inspection may require a qualified electrician, plumber or building team first. A washing-machine booking does not automatically include property wiring, earthing or plumbing correction.
