Make the first response about safety, not a test cycle

Do not switch the washer on to see whether it still works after finding suspected damage. If it is already in use and there is a leak, unusual burning smell, shock or visible damage, stop use and keep away from the affected area. Use an isolation control only when it can be reached safely without approaching the hazard.

Do not reach underneath, remove a cover or handle a damaged cable to improve a photograph. A description from a safe position is enough to begin the enquiry. If the area itself is unsafe, arrange the appropriate urgent help before a routine repair appointment.

Tell the provider whether power or water remains connected, if you know without checking unsafely. It is acceptable to say that you cannot safely establish the current state.

Separate signs of activity from confirmed appliance damage

State exactly what you noticed: visible chew marks on an accessible item, pest activity around the utility area, a leak or a washer that no longer starts. Keep an observation distinct from a conclusion about the damaged part.

A suspected rodent problem can coexist with an unrelated appliance fault. Equally, finding one damaged area does not prove that it is the only affected area. The assessment should establish what can be confirmed within its accepted scope.

If someone has already opened or repaired the washer, provide that history and any photographs they supplied. Do not disturb contaminated material or try to clean inside the appliance yourself; ask the relevant professionals what preparation is appropriate before the service visit.

Agree what the appliance assessment will cover

Ask the provider to identify the accepted inspection task and any conditions that must be addressed before it can proceed. If contamination or active pest activity prevents appropriate access, coordinate that issue first with the relevant service.

The appliance assessment should connect the reported symptom with actual findings. Ask which damage is confirmed, what remains uncertain and whether further access or assessment is needed. A remote photograph should not be treated as a complete internal inspection.

If a property cable, wall connection, tap or drain is also affected, identify it separately. A washing-machine repair does not automatically include the building’s electrical or plumbing work. This distinction helps avoid leaving an external problem unresolved after an appliance component is replaced.

Ask for a repair proposal tied to the damage found

The proposal should describe the affected item, its role in the complaint and the accepted remedy. Ask whether a component is being replaced, an existing assembly is being repaired or further assessment is required before either decision.

Do not assume that every suspected rodent incident requires a control board, motor or complete wiring replacement. Those decisions need findings. At the same time, the cheapest visible patch may not address the actual condition or allow appropriate completion checks.

Do not accept improvised joins, tape-only fixes, altered safety devices or trial operation with damaged parts as a household workaround. Ask the professional to explain a suitable repair for the exact model and to identify anything that makes restoration impractical.

Confirm compatibility and the complete supply arrangement

If a replacement is recommended, retain the full model and component description in the quote. Brand and capacity alone may not identify the correct hose, harness or assembly. A part that looks similar in an online listing is not automatically suitable.

Ask whether the item is new, an agreed compatible alternative or another condition, and whether availability is confirmed. Keep the proposed condition separate from general descriptions such as original or genuine. The final invoice should describe what was actually supplied.

If sourcing changes the proposed item or timeline, request an update before fitting. Do not purchase a component independently until the intended fitter has accepted the item and the actual work.

Coordinate pest control without confusing it with repair

Repairing the washer addresses the accepted appliance damage. It does not establish that the surrounding pest problem has been resolved. Ask the responsible pest-control or building team to assess the environment and suitable prevention measures.

Tell the appliance provider about any treatment or cleanup already undertaken and any access restrictions that remain. Let each professional explain what must happen before the next task. Do not spray treatment products into appliance openings or conceal suspected damage under a homemade covering.

Where a manufacturer specifies a protective panel for the actual model, ask about its correct identity and condition. A generic mesh, blocked opening or improvised enclosure should not replace model-specific guidance or interfere with the appliance’s operation and service access.

Compare the repair total and the separate property tasks

Request the complete accepted appliance amount: assessment, parts, fitting and any additional access or return visit that has been agreed. The price depends on the model, confirmed damage and work required; rat damage is not one fixed-price repair category.

Keep pest control, cleanup and property correction as separate items where they are provided by others. That makes the total project cost clearer and shows whether an important task is still unassigned.

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out, with inspection charges dependent on location. Confirm the actual terms before booking, including what applies if access or the condition of the area prevents assessment. Approve changes in scope and price before extra work begins.

Review the actual coverage instead of assuming it

If manufacturer coverage or another service plan may still apply, check the relevant provider’s terms before independent work. Do not assume every policy treats suspected pest damage in the same way or that a phone description settles eligibility.

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry the stated one-year warranty under written terms. Ask how those terms apply to the supplied component and what is covered if another incident occurs. Do not interpret the parts warranty as a guarantee against future pest activity.

Keep the finding, component description, invoice and any prevention work together. A later concern should be assessed against the current condition and applicable terms, rather than automatically labelled the same covered fault.

Finish with a clear appliance status and prevention plan

At handover, ask what damage was found, what was repaired and which relevant checks were completed. If the washer is not ready for use or a property dependency remains, record that explicitly.

Ask who is responsible for outstanding pest-control, cleanup or property tasks and whether they need to be completed before ordinary use resumes. A repaired appliance and a completed site-prevention plan are different outcomes.

Retain any existing safe evidence and the written job record. If the symptom returns, describe the new observation and timing rather than opening the machine to compare the repair with old photographs.

Three suspected rat-damage situations

These situations illustrate decisions, not customer testimonials or claims about completed jobs.

Pest activity is visible nearby, but the washer’s fault is unclear

Report both facts. Let the appliance assessment establish whether damage is present and whether it explains the symptom.

An accessible hose looks damaged and water has leaked

Keep away from any electrical hazard and explain the condition before booking. Do not patch the hose and run a trial cycle.

The damaged component is replaced while pest activity continues in the room

Keep the environmental work as an outstanding task with a responsible provider. Appliance repair alone does not complete prevention.

Visit charges and the price of the actual work

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Confirm the applicable booking and inspection amounts, and how they are treated if you approve work, before scheduling. The ₹200 visiting amount is not the price of a complete repair or a promise of a free inspection.

The repair total depends on the brand, full model, diagnosed fault, required part, labour and access. Ask for the complete payable amount before approval, including any separately agreed transport, additional visit or tax. A symptom described on the phone can guide the enquiry but may not support a fixed final price.

Keep the diagnosis and quotation together. If the scope changes after assessment, ask for the revised explanation and total before additional work begins. Any property plumbing or electrical work must be identified and priced separately where required.

New parts, compatibility and your one-year warranty

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. The invoice should identify the covered component and written terms. This is not automatically a warranty on the entire washing machine, every future fault or the property installation.

Ask whether a proposed component is manufacturer-original, a compatible alternative or repair of the existing assembly. These are different offers. Confirm the full model match, condition, sourcing and installed price before approving it. A “universal” description alone does not establish suitability.

Labour, workmanship, repairs to existing components and revisit terms need separate confirmation. Keep the invoice and contact the provider with the original job details if a concern returns. Where manufacturer warranty or a service plan may still apply, check that route before authorising independent work.

Prepare access without opening or moving the machine

Keep the model from your invoice or an existing safe label photograph, the complete error message and any previous repair record ready. Clear ordinary access only where it is safe. Tell us about stairs, a stacked installation, a tight enclosure or a locked utility area before the appointment.

Do not dismantle panels, force a locked door, tilt a heavy washer or recreate a dangerous symptom for a video. If there is a shock, smoke, burning, severe movement or water near electrics, stop use and keep away; arrange the appropriate urgent help before a routine appliance visit. Use an isolation control only if it is safely accessible without approaching the hazard.

Safe inspection may require a qualified electrician, plumber or building team first. A washing-machine booking does not automatically include property wiring, earthing or plumbing correction.

Questions customers ask

Can I enquire about suspected rat damage in Bangalore?

Yes. Send the model, locality and safely observed signs so supported appliance assessment can be discussed.

Should I switch the washer on to check whether it works?

Do not energise suspected damaged equipment just to test it. Arrange appropriate assessment first.

Does seeing a rat nearby prove the washer is damaged?

No. Report the observation, but the condition and cause need assessment.

Should I remove the bottom panel for photographs?

No. Do not dismantle, tilt or reach under the machine to obtain evidence.

Does every case need a new control board?

No. Any replacement should follow a finding that identifies the affected component.

Is pest control included in washing-machine repair?

Not automatically. Arrange the environmental assessment and any cleanup separately.

Can I add a generic mesh or cover?

Do not improvise a covering that could affect operation or access. Check model-specific protection requirements.

What affects the repair price?

The model, confirmed damage, required components, labour, access and separately accepted visits.

Does manufacturer warranty always cover pest damage?

Do not assume coverage or exclusion. Check the actual policy and provider’s assessment.

Does the new-part warranty guarantee rats cannot damage it again?

No such prevention guarantee is made. Ask how the written component terms apply.

What should the handover record state?

Damage found, accepted work, relevant checks, readiness for use and outstanding property or prevention tasks.

What if I find another damaged area later?

Stop use where safety is uncertain and report the new observation with the earlier job record; do not attempt a patch.
