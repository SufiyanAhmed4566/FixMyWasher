A washer that has been stored for months or years should be assessed from its actual history and condition, not restarted simply to see what happens. Explain where it was kept, why it stopped being used and whether it has been reinstalled. Visible damage or possible water exposure changes the appropriate next step.

Start with why the machine stopped being used

A working washer stored during a house move has a different history from one put aside because it was leaking or repeatedly stopping. Tell the provider which situation applies, even if the machine has not been touched for a long time.

Record the last known normal operation and any assessment or repair that was pending before storage. If you inherited the appliance or do not know its earlier condition, say so. Do not describe it as working merely because that was assumed when it was put away.

This history helps define the first question. The provider may need to assess a known unresolved fault, discuss setup after relocation or evaluate a condition that developed while unused. An accurate starting point is more useful than booking a generic deep clean and hoping it covers every concern.

Describe the storage environment without assuming the outcome

Tell the provider whether the washer was kept in a dry indoor room, a damp utility space, a balcony or another setting. Mention any known rain, flooding or nearby leak. Exposure history matters even when the exterior now appears clean.

If water may have entered or affected electrical equipment, do not reconnect the machine as a test. Use the appropriate water-exposure assessment route. If the history is simply unknown, keep that uncertainty in the enquiry rather than treating absence of a visible mark as proof that no exposure occurred.

A long storage period does not by itself establish which component, if any, has failed. The assessment should identify the actual condition and relevant next step rather than assign a standard replacement list based only on elapsed time.

Report obvious damage or pest signs from a safe position

If you can already see cable damage, damaged hoses, an unusual opening or signs of pest activity, tell the provider. Use an ordinary safe view; there is no need to reach behind the machine, remove a panel or clear internal debris yourself.

Do not tape a damaged electrical lead, improvise a connection or apply a chemical treatment inside the appliance. The appropriate professional should assess the affected area and explain whether repair or another step is needed.

Keep the record factual. A visible mark is information for assessment, not proof that all internal systems are damaged or unaffected. If the surrounding storage area also needs separate attention, coordinate that task without treating it as part of an automatically included appliance repair.

Account for the move out of storage

Say who moved the washer, what preparation is known and whether model-specific transport accessories were used or retained. If those details are unavailable, explain that before arranging setup at the new location.

Do not assume a household model has the same transport procedure as another washer. The actual manufacturer instructions should guide preparation and installation. Missing accessories or uncertain handling should be clarified through the appropriate provider rather than corrected with improvised materials.

If the machine is still in storage, describe stairs, tight access and any stacked equipment. The service enquiry does not automatically include carrying or transport. Confirm who handles movement and which task the appliance provider has actually accepted.

Decide what cleaning can and cannot answer

Dust, residue or an odour may prompt a care enquiry, but cleaning is not a substitute for assessing damage, an unresolved fault or uncertain installation. Explain the specific concern rather than requesting every available cleaning task.

Ask what the proposed cleaning scope addresses and whether any separate assessment is needed first. Follow the exact model’s owner-care instructions; do not mix chemicals or apply an aggressive treatment in the hope of restoring an unknown internal condition.

A fresh smell or clean exterior does not prove that the washer is ready for normal use. Keep the condition assessment, care task and functional outcome distinct so the household understands what each part of the visit establishes.

Consider the present installation as well as the stored machine

The washer may now be in a different home or utility area. Tell the provider about its current placement, access and known supply or drainage changes. A concern after reconnection can involve the installation, the appliance or both.

Property electrical and plumbing issues require the appropriate qualified professional and a separately understood scope. Do not alter fixed connections or use an improvised lead to power the machine for a trial.

If an installation task is requested, confirm whether the provider supports it for the exact model and what it includes. A visit described as repair should not be expected to include every task required to bring an unused appliance into a new location.

Agree the return-to-use process through the correct model guidance

Use the actual appliance manual and official manufacturer support for model-specific instructions. The required preparation cannot be determined from a universal storage period or a generic instruction to run one empty cycle.

Tell the provider whether any operation has already been attempted and what happened. Do not repeat a test that produced leaking, severe movement, burning, shock or tripping. Existing observations can be shared without reproducing the condition.

For LG appliances, the official India support hub is a route to relevant product assistance. Other brands should use their own official model documentation. Ask what is established and what remains unknown

A useful finding identifies the relevant condition, proposed next task and any limitation. Ask whether the machine’s known pre-storage complaint was assessed and whether the present setup introduced a separate question.

If safe operation was not possible, keep that limitation explicit. If the machine was checked under only certain conditions, do not interpret the result as proof of performance across every future programme and load.

This page does not promise a safety certificate, remaining-life estimate or complete hidden-defect check. Any formal report requirement should be stated before booking and confirmed as an actual supported scope, rather than added to the meaning of a routine service note afterward.

Compare the total needed to make the appliance useful again

If work is proposed, ask for the complete scope rather than only the first component price. Include any separately agreed installation, transport, fitting, further assessment or other task required for the intended household use.

Consider the machine’s prior condition, actual findings, suitable parts and uncertainty. A stored appliance you already own can still need a substantial decision, and a low initial assessment amount should not be mistaken for the cost of restoring its function.

Start a clear service record for the next period of use

Keep the storage history, assessment finding, work performed and relevant checks together. If a condition remains pending, state the next step and contact. Do not describe an assessment-only visit as a completed repair.

Ask for appropriate model-specific care guidance and retain the invoice and written terms for any supplied component. If another household member will use the washer, share the actual outcome and any agreed limitations.

If a concern appears after the return to use, report the current behaviour and refer to this record. The history makes it easier to distinguish an old unresolved complaint, a setup issue and a later fault without assuming they all have the same cause.

New parts, compatibility and your one-year warranty

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. The invoice should identify the covered component and written terms. This is not automatically a warranty on the entire washing machine, every future fault or the property installation.

Ask whether a proposed component is manufacturer-original, a compatible alternative or repair of the existing assembly. These are different offers. Confirm the full model match, condition, sourcing and installed price before approving it. A “universal” description alone does not establish suitability.

Labour, workmanship, repairs to existing components and revisit terms need separate confirmation. Keep the invoice and contact the provider with the original job details if a concern returns. Where manufacturer warranty or a service plan may still apply, check that route before authorising independent work.

Prepare access without opening or moving the machine

Keep the model from your invoice or an existing safe label photograph, the complete error message and any previous repair record ready. Clear ordinary access only where it is safe. Tell us about stairs, a stacked installation, a tight enclosure or a locked utility area before the appointment.

Do not dismantle panels, force a locked door, tilt a heavy washer or recreate a dangerous symptom for a video. If there is a shock, smoke, burning, severe movement or water near electrics, stop use and keep away; arrange the appropriate urgent help before a routine appliance visit. Use an isolation control only if it is safely accessible without approaching the hazard.

Safe inspection may require a qualified electrician, plumber or building team first. A washing-machine booking does not automatically include property wiring, earthing or plumbing correction.

Questions customers ask

Should I switch on a washer that has been stored for years?

Do not use a trial start to assess suspected damage or water exposure. Describe the history and arrange the appropriate model-specific assessment or setup route.

Does long storage always mean parts must be replaced?

No. Elapsed time alone does not diagnose the actual condition.

What history is most useful?

Why it was stored, last known operation, environment, preparation, movement and current setup.

What if the washer was already faulty before storage?

Keep that complaint and any earlier finding in the enquiry; storage does not resolve it.

Can cleaning alone prove it is ready for use?

No. Care, installation and functional assessment answer different questions.

Should I repair a visibly damaged cable myself?

No. Do not improvise repairs or connections; arrange appropriate professional assessment.

What if water exposure is possible?

Do not reconnect it for a test. Disclose the exposure and use the appropriate evaluation route.

Does the visit include moving it out of storage?

Not automatically. Carrying, transport and access work need separate confirmation.

Is an empty cycle a universal recommissioning method?

No. Follow the exact model guidance and the appropriate assessment for its condition.

Can the assessment guarantee remaining life?

No. It should state findings and limitations rather than promise future lifespan.

What costs should I compare?

The complete agreed assessment, repair, parts, fitting and any separate setup or movement tasks needed.

What should I retain afterward?

Storage history, findings, completed scope, checks, invoice, component details and written terms.
