What the door-seal service covers

The service focuses on the sealing area and the reported leak. The assessment should distinguish damage to the rubber from contamination on the contact surfaces, a door alignment issue or water travelling from elsewhere.

  • Review leak timing, load conditions and visible foam.
  • Inspect the accessible seal, glass contact area and related door condition.
  • Identify whether cleaning or a compatible replacement is appropriate.
  • Fit the approved seal and assess associated retainers or connections as required.
  • Check closure and leakage under suitable operating conditions.

Any additional door, hinge, drum-contact or internal leak work should be explained separately. Replacing the rubber alone is not a promise to correct an unrelated source of water.

When cleaning may be enough

Loose hair, lint or product residue can affect contact between the glass and rubber on applicable machines. An intact seal should not automatically be replaced just because a leak was reported.

Follow the model’s cleaning method for accessible surfaces after safe shutdown. Do not use abrasive tools, strong solvents or sharp objects to clear a crease. Avoid mixing cleaning chemicals.

If the leak continues after permitted cleaning, the next step is inspection rather than repeatedly increasing the cleaning strength. For symptom guidance, see leaking from the door.

Manufacturer reference: LG: debris at the door gasket and leakage. Procedures and parts vary by model; use the manual for your machine.

A damaged seal needs the cause considered too

A trapped zip, sharp object or garment may damage rubber, but the cause should not be guessed from a cut alone. Tell the technician about trapped laundry, unusual rubbing, recent repairs or an altered drum position.

If a new seal would contact another damaged or displaced part, that condition needs assessment before fitting. Otherwise the same symptom may recur even with a new gasket.

Do not patch a working sealing surface with tape, glue or silicone. Do not cut away a loose section to keep using the washer. If the seal is torn or the door leaks during operation, stop use until the appropriate repair is established.

Why the exact seal matters

Door seals vary with model and version. Their shape, mounting profile and any drainage or other connections must match the appliance design. Diameter and visual resemblance are not sufficient evidence of compatibility.

Provide the complete model identification from a receipt or safely accessible label. Ask for the proposed part description and whether associated retainers or damaged fittings need separate attention.

Confirm the source and description of the part before approval. This page does not claim that every part supplied is manufacturer-branded. A new part must be accurately described, compatible and recorded with the repair paperwork.

What replacement involves at a service level

The technician safely isolates the machine and accesses the sealing assembly according to the model. The old seal is removed, relevant seating surfaces and fittings are assessed, and the compatible replacement is positioned and secured correctly.

Access can vary between models, so this is not a customer dismantling guide. Retaining bands, springs and surrounding panels should not be removed simply to compare the rubber with an online image.

If inspection uncovers another problem, the scope and additional cost should be explained before that work proceeds. The final check should address the original leak, not just whether the new seal looks neat.

Foam and other leak sources can change the repair

Report excessive suds and the detergent type and measured amount. A suds problem may accompany a sealing fault or resemble one. Use the foam-overflow guide if bubbles are escaping.

Water from the detergent drawer can travel across the door surround, and water underneath may originate from another connection. A puddle at the front is not enough to prove that a door seal is the cause.

If the origin is unclear, request inspection and diagnosis rather than pre-authorising a gasket. Describe the first wet point you actually saw and say when it is unknown.

Keep a leaking washer stopped

Stop the programme with dry, safely accessible controls. Close accessible supply taps if the leak is substantial or water keeps entering. Keep people away from a slippery floor.

Do not touch wet plugs or sockets. If electrical connections may be affected, have power isolated from a dry location by someone competent. Do not continue a wash with a towel catching the drips.

Keep the door closed if water could spill out or may be hot. Follow the model’s safe draining and release procedure or arrange help; do not force a locked door open to inspect the seal.

Checks after a replacement seal is fitted

The seal should sit as intended without folds or trapped material interfering with the door. Normal closure and the relevant water-containing stages should be assessed for leakage under safe test conditions.

Where related connections or retaining components were disturbed, they also need checking. If the original leak occurred only at a particular stage, that history should guide the verification.

Do not pull on the fitted seal or push against a closed door to test it yourself. Ask the technician to explain the checks completed and any remaining concern, such as a separate alignment or drainage issue.

Examples of different door-rubber decisions

Illustrative situations: These examples explain repair decisions. They are not records of actual FixMyWasher customer jobs.

An intact seal with material on the glass contact area

A customer might notice a small closed-door leak even though the rubber is not torn. If accessible debris is the cause and correct cleaning restores the seal, a replacement may not be needed.

A visible tear in the sealing surface

A washer may have a cut where clothing became trapped. After confirming the damage and checking related door condition, a compatible new gasket may be the appropriate repair.

A replacement would not explain the water trail

Water may first escape at the drawer and then run over the door surround. In that situation, replacing the door rubber would not address the identified source.

Care after the repair

Keep clothing fully inside the drum, check pockets and follow the selected programme’s loading limits. Close the door without pushing bulky laundry into place with the glass.

Use the manufacturer’s cleaning guidance for the rubber and accessible glass. Watch for trapped items and report new rubbing or leakage promptly. Do not add coatings, oils or adhesive covers to the seal as a maintenance shortcut.

Frequently asked questions

Does a leaking front door always need new rubber?

No. Residue, trapped material, alignment and water from another source should be distinguished from a damaged seal.

Can mould marks alone prove the seal needs replacement?

No. Appearance and structural condition are different questions. Follow approved cleaning guidance and have damaged or persistently leaking rubber assessed.

Can I repair a tear with glue or silicone?

Do not improvise a patch on the sealing surface. Have the correct repair assessed and keep the leaking appliance stopped.

Are all front-loader door seals the same?

No. Model, version, shape and connection requirements matter. A similar-looking seal is not enough to establish compatibility.

What if the leak started after a previous seal replacement?

Report the timing and original work. Fit, related connections, door condition and the actual water source may need reassessment.

What do I pay if I do not have the repair done?

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location; confirm all applicable charges before the visit.

Is the new seal supplied by FixMyWasher warranted?

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Confirm the seal details and labour or revisit arrangements with the paperwork.

Can I keep using the machine if only a few drops escape?

Stop a closed-door leak and establish its cause. The visible amount does not show where else water may be reaching.
