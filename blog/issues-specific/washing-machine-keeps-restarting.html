Washing machine keeps restarting: power reset or a repeating cycle?

First check what “restarting” looks like. Does the display go blank and return to its startup screen, or does it stay on while the washer repeats rinsing or tries to spin again? These are different symptoms and need different checks.

Record the sequence you have already seen, any error message and whether the washer eventually finishes. This guide helps you identify useful clues without opening the appliance or performing electrical tests.

Stop using the washer if restarting comes with warning signs

Stop for a burning smell, smoke, sparking, a hot or damaged plug, repeated circuit trips, severe banging or water near electrical connections. Do not wait for another restart to confirm the problem.

Use the normal stop controls if safe. Avoid wet plugs and switches; have electricity isolated safely from a dry location by someone competent. If leaking water can be stopped using a safely accessible supply tap, close it.

Do not keep resetting a tripped breaker. Do not try different sockets, extensions or adapters to keep the washer running. A supply problem and an appliance fault need proper assessment.

Never bypass a door or lid lock, open electrical panels or hold a violently moving washer in place.

A beep, a click or the sound of a pump is not enough to identify the cause. The display behaviour and order of events are more useful.

If the display turns off and starts up again

A washer repeatedly losing its display needs the electrical supply and the appliance considered separately. A visible restart does not prove that the main circuit board is faulty.

Report whether lights or other equipment also lost power, whether a circuit tripped and whether there was a building power changeover. These observations help direct the investigation, but they do not establish a diagnosis.

Do not wiggle a plug while the machine runs or open a socket to inspect it. Leave checking the outlet, circuit and supply to a qualified electrician. Appliance wiring and internal power or control faults require a competent repair technician.

If an extension, adapter or external power accessory is in use, tell the provider. Follow the washer’s electrical installation requirements; do not purchase a stabiliser or replacement board solely because the machine restarts.

Manufacturer reference: Samsung guidance on unexpected shutdown and power issues. Apply the electrical requirements for your exact model and country.

If the display stays on but wash stages repeat

Repeated attempts to spin

Some washers redistribute an uneven load before trying to spin again. Depending on the model, this can involve further movement or water intake. It can look like the programme is starting over even though the control panel never rebooted.

Note whether it mainly happens with a single bulky item or an unsuitable load. Follow the manual and care labels. Adjust laundry only after motion has stopped and safe opening is permitted; do not force the door.

If suitable loads repeatedly cannot finish, see the not spinning guide and arrange assessment.

Rinsing repeats and foam remains

On some models, excess suds can extend rinsing. Record the detergent type and dose, then follow the manufacturer’s instructions. Do not add more detergent, dishwashing liquid or other chemicals to resolve the loop.

Water never seems to clear

Report retained water and any drain error. A cycle struggling to drain needs a different investigation from a power reset. Use the not draining guide.

References: LG guidance on imbalance and repeated balancing and Samsung guidance on changing cycle time. Behaviour varies between models.

What if it restarts after a power cut?

A restart after a known power interruption should be assessed against the model’s recovery instructions. Some machines can resume an interrupted programme; others need user action. Do not assume every washer must return to the beginning or continue automatically.

Once supply is stable and there are no warning signs, follow the manual’s resume procedure. Avoid adding another detergent dose to a wash that already contains it.

If the appliance repeatedly resets after the household interruption has ended, report that separately. A one-off recovery and an ongoing restart loop are different problems.

Save error messages and describe unexpected control behaviour

Photograph the complete display before any permitted reset. Record letters, numbers and symbols exactly; similar codes can mean different things across brands and models. Use the display error guide for recording the message.

If programme selection changes, note whether someone pressed a button or used a connected app. If settings change without an explained command, describe which controls are affected rather than assuming the entire board needs replacement.

Do not spray cleaner into the panel, press buttons repeatedly or enter service menus. Follow the manual for exterior cleaning and control-lock settings. A control-lock symbol by itself does not establish the cause of restarting.

Useful observations to collect safely

  1. Model and machine type: use a receipt or safely accessible label; do not move the washer to find it.
  2. Display sequence: say whether it goes blank, shows startup lights or remains on.
  3. Programme stage: note filling, washing, draining or the beginning of spin.
  4. Power context: mention a known outage, changeover or circuit trip.
  5. Load and detergent: include bulky items, visible foam and whether similar loads previously completed.
  6. Recent changes: report installation work, relocation or an earlier repair.
  7. Outcome: say whether it finishes eventually, stops with water inside or requires repeated intervention.

Existing photos or a safely recorded clip can help. Do not restart an unsafe appliance or leave it running to gather evidence.

Should you reset a washer that keeps restarting?

First record the symptom and check for warning signs. Only use a reset or restart procedure specified for the exact model when the machine and supply are safe. There is no universal button combination or unplugging interval for all brands.

If the same problem returns, stop repeating the procedure and arrange diagnosis. A temporary recovery does not confirm that the fault has been repaired.

A reset is not a remedy for damaged electrical connections, recurring circuit trips or leaking water. Do not use an internet service-mode sequence to clear evidence of a persistent problem.

What about water inside and a locked door?

Keep the door closed if water could escape or the wash water may be hot. Switching power off does not guarantee immediate unlocking.

Follow only the model’s safe draining and emergency opening procedure. Do not unscrew a filter on a full or hot machine without the required draining steps. Arrange help if access or the procedure is unclear.

See the door not opening guide. Tell the provider that water remains inside so they can plan safe access.

What should a repair assessment cover?

The first task is to establish whether power is being interrupted, the appliance is resetting or the programme is retrying a stage. The next checks should follow that evidence.

  • Confirm the model, sequence and any stored or displayed faults.
  • Review supply and installation concerns, involving an electrician where needed.
  • Check relevant drain, drive, sensing, safety or control systems as indicated.
  • Explain the findings before recommending replacement parts.
  • Verify the affected operating stages after agreed work.

For a confirmed control fault, see our circuit board repair guide. Restarting alone is not evidence that a board must be replaced.

Clarify the quote before work begins

Ask about inspection charges, parts, labour, what applies if you decline repair and any repair warranty terms. Confirm whether household electrical or plumbing work is included. If manufacturer warranty may apply, review its conditions before outside repairs.

Questions about repeated washer restarts

Is repeated rinsing the same as restarting?

Not necessarily. If the display stays on, the washer may be repeating an operating stage. Note the load, foam, drainage and any message.

Does a reboot mean the circuit board is faulty?

No. Supply interruptions and appliance faults need distinguishing. A parts recommendation should follow diagnosis.

Can an unbalanced load make the cycle seem to start again?

On relevant models, repeated balancing attempts can involve more movement or water intake. Follow the load guidance and arrange assessment if suitable loads repeatedly fail.

Should I buy a stabiliser?

Do not choose electrical accessories based on this symptom alone. Check the washer’s installation requirements and have suspected supply problems assessed.

Can I keep resetting a tripped breaker?

No. Stop using the appliance and arrange proper assessment. Repeated tripping needs investigation before further operation.

Why did it restart after a power cut?

Power-failure recovery differs by model. Follow the manual; report repeated resets that continue after the supply interruption has ended.

Should I add detergent when the cycle resumes?

Do not automatically add another dose to laundry that already contains detergent. Follow the model’s recovery instructions.

What should I send when requesting repair?

The model, display sequence, affected stage, exact error and whether other equipment lost power. Include your Bangalore locality and any safety warning signs.
