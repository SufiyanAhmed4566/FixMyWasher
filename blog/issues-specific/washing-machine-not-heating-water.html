Washing machine not heating water: normal behaviour or a heating fault?

Cold laundry at the end of a wash does not by itself prove that the machine failed to heat. Rinsing can cool the load, some programmes use cold water, and not every washer has an internal heater.

Start with the exact model, selected programme and temperature setting. A recurring heating error, a new interruption or a confirmed failure to reach the programme’s intended conditions needs a different response from clothes that simply feel cool when unloaded.

Do not open the washer during a hot programme to check the water. Avoid deliberately touching hot glass, inserting thermometers through openings or collecting hot drain water for a test.

Confirm whether your model heats water internally

Find the heating and water-connection information in the exact model manual. Some washers heat incoming cold water internally; some use hot and cold supply connections; others do not offer internally heated wash programmes.

“Fully automatic”, brand name or loading style alone is not enough to establish the heating arrangement. A temperature selector or steam feature also needs to be interpreted using that model’s instructions.

Do not buy a heating element before confirming that one belongs to the wash system of your model. On a washer-dryer, drying heat and wash-water heating are separate functions to describe, even when the controls share one panel.

Check the actual programme and temperature choice

Record the programme name and selected temperature before starting or from an existing photo. Options can vary by programme, and a temperature you used previously may not be selected for the current wash.

A cold wash, rinse-only sequence or another programme without water heating should not be expected to behave like a heated cotton programme. Follow fabric-care labels before choosing a warmer setting; do not use delicate clothing for a high-temperature test.

If an app is involved, check the selection shown by the appliance and the saved programme details. Do not assume the number on the display is a live reading of the water temperature; it may show a setting or time instead.

Why laundry can be cold after a heated wash

The wash and rinse stages can use different water temperatures. On applicable models, cold rinsing follows a heated wash, so the final load can feel cool even though heating occurred earlier.

Do not use the temperature of unloaded clothes as the only basis for a heater replacement. Similarly, the outside glass or cabinet does not provide a dependable measurement of the water inside.

If the only concern is cool laundry after an otherwise normal programme, check the manual’s sequence first. If there is also an error, stalled programme or other change, report that additional evidence.

Eco programme names need careful interpretation

On models with programmes such as Eco 40–60, the name should not be treated as a promise that the outside of the machine will feel like a conventional hot wash. The programme may manage temperature and time differently to meet its intended washing performance and efficiency.

Read the model’s programme table and its temperature notes. Do not compare two programmes solely by how warm the glass feels, or assume a long eco cycle means the heater is faulty.

If performance has changed on the same familiar programme, record that history. The comparison is useful context, but the heating system still needs proper assessment before a specific part can be blamed.

Water supply matters on models that use a hot inlet

If your manual specifies both hot and cold connections, report any recent installation work, supply interruption or change to the household hot-water system. The technician may need to check that the correct supply reaches the correct inlet.

Do not swap hoses while they are pressurised, connect a cold-only washer to hot water or pour kettle water into the drum or drawer. Follow the appliance’s permitted supply temperatures and connection requirements.

A cold-fill washer with an internal heater has a different setup from one relying on a hot inlet. No hot-water connection should be added simply because the laundry feels cold.

Errors and interrupted cycles provide more useful evidence

Photograph the complete error message and note whether it appears during filling, washing, rinsing or drying. Code meanings vary across models, so do not use a code explanation from a different machine as a confirmed diagnosis.

If a programme is not progressing, also report slow filling, retained water, unusual sounds or power interruptions. These observations can change the repair path. Use stops mid-cycle or cycle taking too long for those symptoms.

Do not repeatedly reset the appliance or enter hidden service menus to force heating. Persistent errors should be assessed using the manufacturer’s procedure.

Poor cleaning alone does not identify a heater fault

Report a change in washing results, but also check the load size, fabric type, programme and detergent use. A stain remaining after one cycle is not a temperature measurement, and increasing the wash temperature is not suitable for every fabric or stain.

If the manual permits a routine cleaning or descaling programme, follow its product and dosing instructions when the appliance is otherwise operating safely. Descaling is maintenance, not proof that a failed heating system has been repaired.

Do not add stronger chemicals or keep running the hottest programme to diagnose the heater. A recurring fault needs testing rather than a series of cleaning experiments.

When to stop using the washer

Stop for burning smells, smoke, sparks, a hot or damaged plug, repeated circuit trips, leakage near electrical connections or unexpected overheating. Do not reset a breaker repeatedly or switch to a cold programme as a workaround for an electrical fault.

Keep the door closed if the contents may be hot or water could spill. Follow the model’s safe cooling and draining procedure or arrange help. Avoid wet electrical fittings and have power isolated safely from a dry location where needed.

Do not open the cabinet to test heater terminals, bridge a temperature sensor or bypass a thermal safety device. These checks belong to a qualified technician.

What a heating repair assessment should cover

The technician should first establish whether heating is expected on the selected programme and how the model provides it. Where a fault is suspected, the assessment may involve the heater, temperature sensing, wiring, controls and relevant supply conditions.

Ask which finding supports the proposed repair. A heating-related message should not automatically lead to a new heater or control board without checking the affected system.

Confirm inspection fees, model-compatible parts, labour and warranty terms. Ask how correct heating and electrical safety will be verified after work. Touching the glass or checking whether clothes feel warm is not an adequate repair test.

Frequently asked questions

Do cold clothes after washing prove the heater is broken?

No. Cold rinsing can cool the load after a heated wash. Check the programme sequence and any error or change in operation.

Does every automatic washing machine have a heater?

No. Heating capability and water connections depend on the model. Check its manual before assuming an internal heating fault.

Can I test heating by touching the door?

Do not rely on external touch as a temperature test, and avoid hot glass. Proper assessment does not require you to touch or open a hot appliance.

Can I pour hot water in to help it heat?

No. Do not add kettle water or change supply connections as a workaround. Follow the model’s installation and operating instructions.

Does Eco 40–60 always behave like a conventional 60°C wash?

No. Programme temperature and timing depend on the design. Read the model’s programme notes rather than judging by the name or door warmth.

Will descaling repair a failed heater?

Descaling is not a confirmed repair for a heating fault. Use approved maintenance only when safe, and have persistent faults assessed.

Can I use a cold wash if heated cycles trip the power?

Do not use another setting to bypass an electrical warning. Stop use and arrange qualified assessment.

My washer-dryer washes normally but does not dry. Is this the same fault?

Not necessarily. Report the drying failure separately because drying and wash-water heating involve different functions.
