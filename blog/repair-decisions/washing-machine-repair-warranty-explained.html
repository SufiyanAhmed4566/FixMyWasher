Washing machine repair warranty explained

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. That is a promise about the new supplied part, not a statement that the entire washing machine has been renewed or covered. A repaired original PCB, retained motor, cleaning service, labour and revisit arrangements need their own actual terms.

Before approving a repair, identify what is being supplied and ask how a concern will be reviewed afterward. This guide explains those questions without inventing extra exclusions, automatic free visits, refund promises or a claim outcome before the fault has been assessed.

Start with what the warranty actually refers to

Ask who provides the promise and which job record identifies it. A warranty period without a named part or scope is incomplete as a practical explanation. A longer period can sound reassuring while leaving the actual responsibility unclear.

Identify the supplied part on the repair record

The record should name the component and show whether it was new, repaired or retained. For an assembly, clarify the included items. A new pump is different from clearing the existing pump path, and a new PCB is different from repairing the original board.

If several items are involved, do not use one vague phrase such as “machine repaired” to describe all of them. The new-part promise should be understandable for each supplied new item. A retained component does not become new simply because it was removed and refitted during access.

Keep any available part reference or supplier description with the approved quotation. You do not need to remove the installed component to read its markings later. The invoice, job record and service provider’s identification should make the supplied scope clear without unsafe inspection.

Confirm the dates instead of assuming how the period runs

Ask the provider to record the start and end dates or clearly state the event from which the period is counted. A quotation date, part-order date, fitting date and invoice date can differ. This guide does not invent which of those controls an individual agreement.

If a warranty-related part is later repaired or replaced, ask what coverage applies afterward. Do not assume that the entire original period restarts or that no cover remains. Record the actual arrangement with the follow-up job details.

Report a concern promptly when it appears and retain the dated communication. Prompt reporting helps preserve a clear history; it does not establish eligibility by itself or replace the provider’s assessment of the problem and terms.

A repaired original needs a separate explanation

Repairing a board or rewinding a suitable motor is work on an existing component. That may be a useful option, but it is not automatically supply of a new complete part. Ask which repair scope and support terms are being offered before choosing it.

Where a small new item is used within a larger repair, have the provider explain what is new and what the overall repair terms mean. Do not assume that one replaced item gives the entire retained assembly a new-part warranty unless that scope is actually stated.

Compare options with this distinction visible. A lower-priced original-component repair and a new replacement can both deserve consideration where technically suitable, but their work, checks and warranty may differ. Price alone cannot tell you which agreement you are being offered.

These are questions to resolve, not a list of invented exclusions. Do not automatically assume that every item is charged, or that every item is free. Obtain the actual terms and keep them with the part warranty statement.

The standard ₹200 visiting charge if no repair is carried out should not be used as an automatic answer to every warranty-related attendance. Confirm how the particular concern will be handled before booking the review.

A returning symptom needs assessment of its cause

The same observed problem can have more than one cause. A washer failing to drain after pump work may need a review of the supplied part, the earlier correction, an obstruction or another relevant condition. The symptom alone does not decide which explanation is correct.

Equally, a provider should not dismiss a recurrence as unrelated without an explanation of the findings. Ask what was assessed and how it relates to the earlier work. The useful distinction is evidence about the current cause, not a reflex assumption in favour of either a free overhaul or another paid part.

Stop using the machine for serious leakage, electrical warnings or severe mechanical contact. Do not repeatedly operate it or open the cabinet to prove a claim. Existing observations and the original job record are enough to start a review.

Prepare a clear warranty concern report

Provide the job reference, repair date, model, supplied-part description and the current symptom. State whether the machine worked normally after the job and when the issue appeared. Include a displayed message or existing photo if it was obtained safely.

Describe facts separately from your suspicion. “Water remains after the programme” is useful; “the new pump is definitely defective” is a conclusion that may still need assessment. You can explain why you are concerned about the earlier repair without claiming a diagnosis you do not have.

Ask for acknowledgement and the proposed next step, including attendance arrangements and any charge treatment. Keep the response with the earlier documents so the history remains understandable if another team member handles the follow-up.

Three examples of how scope changes the conversation

Illustrative situations: These examples explain repair decisions. They are not records of actual FixMyWasher customer jobs.

A newly supplied pump and a later drainage complaint

Suppose FixMyWasher supplied a new pump and the machine later stops draining. Report the job and symptom for assessment against the supplied-part warranty and actual service terms. The complaint deserves review, but it does not automatically establish that the pump failed or that every possible drainage repair is free.

An original PCB was repaired rather than replaced

Suppose the approved ₹2,800 option was repair of the existing board. The one-year new-part promise should not be presented as a warranty on a newly supplied complete PCB. Review the separately agreed original-board repair terms and the current findings.

A new seal with another retained component concern

Suppose a new door seal was fitted and a different component later needs work. The seal warranty does not by itself extend to that retained part. Ask whether the current problem relates to the supplied seal or a separate fault, then review the applicable scope before approving any new charge.

Separate this promise from manufacturer cover

A manufacturer policy concerns the appliance or specified components under that manufacturer’s terms. FixMyWasher’s warranty concerns new parts it supplies in its own work. One does not automatically replace, extend or administer the other.

Samsung India’s official warranty page, for example, distinguishes product cover from named motor cover and purchase-period terms. That illustrates why the longest number printed in marketing is not a complete coverage explanation for every repair. Use your applicable documents and official claim route.

If manufacturer cover may remain, check the effect of proposed third-party work before authorising dismantling or repair. Do not assume manufacturer authorisation for FixMyWasher or that an independent repair creates a right to claim through the manufacturer.

Where to check the other policy

For manufacturer claims, use the appliance maker’s official support and the terms applicable to your model and purchase. Keep its service reference separate from an independent repair record so the provider and responsibility are clear.

Read repair under manufacturer warranty for the documents and booking questions to prepare. A comparison page can help you ask questions, but it cannot approve a claim on a manufacturer’s behalf.

Keep a small, usable set of documents

Save the original quote, approved changes, invoice or job record, supplied-part description, completion checks and written warranty terms. Keep a payment record and the contact route for follow-up. These items should explain what happened without relying on someone’s memory.

A brief completion note can state which fault was addressed and whether testing had limitations. If a long programme or intermittent symptom could not be fully checked, that information matters to a later review. It should not disappear behind an unqualified “all okay” message.

For workshop work, retain the handover and return record too. If the same item is sent for review, connect the new reference to the earlier job. A clear chain of records helps distinguish original repair work from later approved changes.

If the terms or claim response are unclear

Ask the provider to identify the item, finding and term being applied. A response such as “not covered” is more useful when it explains the scope or actual cause rather than merely repeats a conclusion. Correct any factual error with the relevant record.

If there is disagreement, keep the repair documents and written communication together and use the provider’s stated review route. This page does not decide legal rights, impose a new claims process or promise a refund. The immediate practical goal is a clear, documented explanation.

Before another business dismantles the machine, consider the existing review and any applicable policy terms. A second opinion can help, but its scope and potential effect on an unresolved claim should be understood beforehand. Do not conceal previous work from either provider.

FixMyWasher’s confirmed warranty statement

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Ask for the actual warranty dates, supplied-item identification and applicable claim arrangements on the job record. This article explains the confirmed promise; it does not add unagreed exclusions or extend it to the entire washer.

Visiting charges and a warranty-related enquiry

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Share your Bangalore locality or PIN code and confirm all applicable charges together before booking. ₹200 is not an advertised complete repair or parts-replacement price.

Ask how the visiting and inspection charges are treated if a repair proceeds. No automatic waiver, deduction or additional charge should be assumed. Your approved quote should make clear whether these amounts are included, credited or payable separately, and state any applicable tax and the final payable total.

For a reported concern about an earlier job, provide the reference first and confirm the particular attendance arrangements. Do not infer a universal warranty revisit fee or waiver from the standard no-repair booking statement.

Discuss a concern about a supplied new part

Contact FixMyWasher with the earlier job details, Bangalore locality, model and current symptom. Explain what changed after the repair and whether any safety warning is present. A photo of the invoice or supplied-part description can help identify the original scope.

Ask how the concern will be assessed and which terms apply before further work. Keep the review connected to the earlier job so that any new findings or separately approved repair are clearly recorded.

Frequently asked questions

What warranty does FixMyWasher give on new parts?

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Identify the supplied item and confirm the recorded dates and separate service or claim arrangements.

Does that cover the whole washing machine?

No. Supplying one new component does not renew or automatically cover all retained components. An assembly should have its actual included items identified.

Is a repaired original PCB covered as a new board?

Not automatically. Repair of an existing board is a different scope. Confirm its own repair terms rather than applying the new-complete-part promise to it.

Are labour and revisits always free during the year?

Do not assume that from the part period alone. Ask for the actual labour, workmanship, attendance and claim terms. This guide does not invent a universal charge or waiver.

When does the one-year period start?

Have the provider record the dates or the event from which coverage is counted. Do not assume that the quote, order, fitting and invoice dates are interchangeable.

Does a replacement during a claim restart the warranty?

Do not infer a restart. Ask for the actual coverage after the follow-up work and keep that statement with the new job record.

Does the same symptom prove the supplied part failed?

No. It warrants assessment against the earlier work and current cause. Similar symptoms can have different causes, so the findings should determine the applicable scope.

What documents should I keep?

Keep the quotation, approved changes, invoice or job record, supplied-part details, dates, checks, warranty terms, payment record and follow-up contact.

Is this the manufacturer’s warranty?

No. FixMyWasher’s supplied-part promise is separate. Use the appliance manufacturer’s official channel and applicable policy for its warranty claims.

What should I do when a covered-part concern appears?

Report it promptly with the job reference and symptom, stop unsafe operation and ask for the assessment and attendance arrangements before further work.
