Should you repair a 10-year-old washing machine?

A ten-year-old washing machine can still be worth repairing when it has one confirmed, suitable repair and otherwise meets your needs. Ten years is a reason to review its condition and repair history carefully, not an automatic expiry date. Replacement deserves a closer look when several essential repairs, substantial damage or unavailable compatible parts make continued ownership less practical.

The right question is what your next payment will achieve. A modest correction on a useful older washer is different from paying for an uncertain major repair on a machine with several unresolved problems. This guide helps you compare those situations without promising extra years or dismissing a repair solely because of age.

Ten calendar years do not describe ten years of use

Two washers bought in the same year may have very different histories. One may serve a small household a few times per week, while another handles frequent loads. Installation, loading habits, care, storage and previous faults also affect the context in which you judge a repair.

If you do not know the purchase date, use the best information you have and label it approximate. A sales invoice or old service record may help; a model number alone should not be treated as a precise purchase date. You do not need an exact birthday to request a useful diagnosis.

Avoid statements such as “all washers fail after ten years” or “this brand always lasts twenty”. Those claims do not establish the condition of your machine. The more useful record is whether it performed its normal tasks before this fault, which repairs it has had and what problems remain.

Make a short repair history before comparing quotes

Write down the current complaint and any earlier work you remember. Distinguish a past fault that was corrected from a symptom that never fully went away. A pump changed years ago with normal use since then is a different situation from three recent unsuccessful attempts to resolve the same interruption.

Keep receipts and messages showing supplied parts and warranty terms. If a symptom has returned soon after a repair, review that job with the provider before paying for the same work again. The return of a symptom does not by itself prove the same part has failed, but it makes the earlier findings relevant.

Do not count earlier spending as a reason you must continue. Money already paid cannot be recovered simply by approving another job. Judge the additional amount you can still choose to spend and the result that the new scope is intended to provide.

Assess what will remain after the proposed work

Ask which concern is being corrected and whether any other essential fault has been identified. The inspection may be limited, so “nothing else found during these checks” is more realistic than a promise that nothing else can fail. Known limitations should be recorded plainly.

Separate cosmetic wear from a concern affecting safe or useful operation. Faded buttons or a scratched panel do not have the same implication as damage affecting a support or mounting point. If corrosion or structural condition is cited as a reason not to repair, ask what it affects.

Your household needs matter too. A repaired washer that remains too small, difficult to access or unsuitable for the laundry you regularly wash may not solve your main problem. Conversely, familiar controls and a good fit in the available space can be legitimate reasons to keep an otherwise useful machine.

Build two complete totals before deciding

For repair, include the agreed corrective work, other confirmed essential work and the stated treatment of inspection, access, transport and tax. Check that parts already included in an assembly and shared labour have not been counted twice.

For replacement, choose a washer you would actually buy, with appropriate capacity and dimensions. Include confirmed delivery, installation, necessary site work and removal arrangements, then subtract only an exchange credit that has genuinely been offered on applicable terms. A headline online price alone can leave that total incomplete.

The comparison should help you choose, not create an automatic percentage rule. Repair cost as a share of a new machine’s price does not explain diagnosis quality, remaining condition or part supply. Keep optional upgrades separate so they do not distort the basic repair-versus-replacement decision.

Three older washers, three different decisions

Illustrative situations: These examples explain repair decisions. They are not records of actual FixMyWasher customer jobs.

A ten-year-old washer with a modest confirmed fault

Suppose a suitable minor correction is quoted at ₹900 and the machine otherwise meets the household’s needs. Repair may be reasonable once the complete payable amount is confirmed. Its age alone does not require disposal, and the low quote does not promise any particular number of remaining years.

A major repair on a machine with another unresolved problem

Suppose an ₹11,000 assembly proposal would still leave a separately confirmed electronic fault unresolved. Compare the combined necessary scope with a suitable replacement, not just the ₹11,000 line. If the second fault has not been diagnosed, establish it before treating another expensive part as certain.

A cheaper repair that does not solve the household’s needs

Suppose the washer can be restored at a modest cost, but the household has already outgrown its capacity. Repair remains an option for keeping that machine working; replacement may serve the broader need. Decide openly which objective you are paying for rather than describing every upgrade as an essential repair.

Part availability can matter more than the age on paper

A ten-year-old model may or may not have a suitable part readily available. Confirm the full model and the proposed component instead of assuming that all older machines lack spares. A similar-looking part from the same brand is not enough evidence of compatibility.

Ask whether the proposal uses a new part, repairs an original component or involves another clearly stated condition of item. Those options have different scopes and potentially different terms. An unspecified “replacement” should not be assumed to mean new.

If a part must be ordered, clarify availability, expected updates and any actual deposit or cancellation arrangements. An estimated supply time is not a confirmed completion date. If no suitable option can be identified, that limitation belongs in the repair decision before you commit to further spending.

Do not buy a promised number of extra years

A technician can explain a fault, repair method and checks, but changing one component does not establish how long all retained components will last. A guarantee of several extra years for the whole washer needs a real written basis, not a reassuring phrase.

A one-year warranty on a supplied new part is also different from an estimate of appliance life. A part can be covered without the entire machine being renewed. Confirm which items are new and what terms apply to labour, repaired originals and revisits.

For broader context, use the washing machine lifespan guide. A general published lifespan estimate can support replacement planning, but it cannot tell you how many years remain in your individual ten-year-old washer.

Assess newer-machine savings with actual numbers

Do not assume that any new washer will repay its purchase through lower bills. A useful comparison needs the selected model’s information, comparable programmes, your loading pattern and applicable electricity and water charges. A different capacity or programme can change the comparison.

If reliable comparable figures are available, annual electricity cost can be estimated as energy per cycle multiplied by cycles per year and the applicable energy charge. State the assumptions. If those inputs are unknown, leave the saving unquantified instead of inventing a convenient payback period.

Convenience, capacity and programme suitability may justify replacement without a financial saving claim. Keeping those reasons explicit makes the choice easier to review and avoids overstating what a label or a newer model year proves.

Agree a limit if further investigation is needed

Before substantial dismantling, establish the assessment charge, purpose, stopping point and what happens if you decline repair afterward. The ₹200 no-repair visiting term does not automatically describe every separately agreed inspection or workshop charge.

If the findings change the scope, request the revised explanation and total before further work. A conditional bearing option becoming an assembly replacement is a new decision, even if the technician has already attended. Agree reassembly and return arrangements where relevant.

Stop use if there is serious leakage, electrical warning, burning smell or severe mechanical contact. Do not keep running an unsafe machine to postpone replacement shopping or demonstrate the problem. Seek appropriate help and compare options once the immediate concern is controlled.

What the one-year new-parts warranty means

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

The age of the washer does not turn the new-part warranty into whole-machine cover. Identify the supplied item and keep its job record.

Frequently asked questions

Is ten years too old to repair a washing machine?

Not automatically. A defined repair can be worthwhile if the machine remains suitable and no major unresolved concerns undermine the work. Use the actual fault and condition.

Should I replace it simply because one part failed?

No. Determine what failed, why the proposed work is suitable and whether other essential faults remain. One part failure is not proof that the whole appliance is beyond repair.

What if the repair costs half as much as a new washer?

That percentage is only one comparison. Review complete totals, diagnosis, part supply, remaining condition and household needs rather than using a universal cut-off.

Do earlier repair bills mean I should keep repairing?

No. They describe the history, but money already spent is not recovered by spending more. Judge the next decision and review any relevant previous warranty.

Can parts still be available for an older model?

Possibly. Availability and compatibility must be checked for the exact model and component; age alone does not answer the question.

Will a repair give my washer another five years?

No precise whole-machine life extension follows from a component repair. Ask for actual scope and warranty rather than relying on a predicted number of years.

Should I buy a new machine for lower electricity bills?

Compare the actual models and usage if that is your reason. Age alone does not establish a guaranteed saving or purchase payback.

Can a ten-year-old washer receive the new-part warranty?

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Confirm the particular supplied part and separate terms for labour, repaired originals and revisits.

What do I pay if I decide not to repair?

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Confirm the complete no-repair amount and any further agreed assessment scope before booking.

What information helps assess an older washing machine?

Provide the model, approximate purchase age, symptom, repair history, locality and any existing quote. Mention other known problems and whether the machine still meets your needs.
