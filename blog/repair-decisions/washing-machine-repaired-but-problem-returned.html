Washing machine repaired but the problem returned: what next?

Contact the original provider with the repair record and the returning symptom before approving another unconfirmed part replacement. Ask for assessment of how the present problem relates to the earlier work and which actual warranty or service terms apply. A similar symptom deserves review, but it does not automatically prove the same component failed.

If the washer leaks significantly, trips electrical protection, smells burnt, gives a shock or makes severe mechanical contact, stop using it and arrange appropriate help. Do not repeatedly recreate the event to produce evidence. This guide helps you organise a useful review and decide whether further diagnosis, corrective work or a separate repair is justified.

If there is active fire or immediate danger, contact emergency help rather than waiting for a routine repair appointment. Isolate a supply only if it can be reached safely from a dry position. Do not touch wet electrical connections or open the appliance.

Write a short timeline before calling

Note the original complaint, date of the repair, work recorded and what happened afterward. Did the machine complete normal programmes for a period, fail on the first use or continue showing the same unresolved symptom? These are different histories worth reporting.

Include the programme stage, displayed message and whether water or laundry remained inside, where those details are already known. State any recent move, installation change or other service work. Do not run a new test solely to fill every field in the timeline.

Use approximate timings if that is all you remember. A clear statement such as “it worked for several ordinary washes and then stopped draining again” is more useful than an invented exact cycle count. Keep observations separate from a proposed explanation.

The same symptom is not a complete diagnosis

A failure to spin can involve several operating conditions or faults. A stopped programme or leak can likewise have more than one cause. Reappearance of the complaint is relevant evidence, but the provider still needs to establish the present cause and its relationship to the earlier repair.

Possible explanations can include a problem with supplied work or a part, an unresolved condition, an intermittent fault that was not reproduced, or a separate cause. Listing those possibilities does not establish any of them in your machine. Ask what the findings support.

Do not let uncertainty become an automatic excuse to dismiss the earlier job. A useful review explains what was checked and why the current proposal follows. It should neither blame the customer without evidence nor promise that every subsequent fault is covered by one prior repair.

Read the earlier scope before comparing another quotation

Check whether the approved work was diagnosis only, general service, cleaning, repair of an original part or supply of a new part. A maintenance visit and a confirmed fault correction are different scopes even if both were called service during the booking.

Identify the supplied component and any retained items or testing limits recorded. If the invoice is vague, ask the provider to explain what was actually done. A replacement of one part does not establish that every other possible cause was ruled out indefinitely.

Keep the original quotation, approved changes and completion checks together. That record can show whether the present concern relates to an unresolved observation or a genuinely new event. It also prevents a revised price from being compared against a scope that was never approved.

Do not send unnecessary household or financial information to explain an appliance fault. The model, job record and relevant observations are usually the useful starting point. If a photo contains unrelated personal details, keep the report focused on the appliance and repair.

What a useful follow-up assessment should explain

Ask whether the original complaint can be reproduced safely and which current findings relate to the earlier work. The technician should explain whether the proposed correction concerns the supplied part, workmanship, a retained condition or a separate fault, to the extent that can be established.

If more investigation is needed, define its purpose and stopping point. An intermittent fault may not appear during a short visit; that limitation should be recorded honestly. A non-reproduction is not the same as proof that the customer’s report was wrong.

If the provider suggests another component, ask what new evidence supports it and why the earlier correction was insufficient or addresses a different cause. That explanation is more useful than replacing a sequence of expensive parts without a clear diagnostic reason.

Check the actual warranty and attendance arrangements

For FixMyWasher, new supplied parts carry a one-year warranty. A repaired original component or cleaning service has a different scope whose terms need confirmation. The entire washer is not automatically covered because one part was supplied.

Ask how the reported recurrence will be assessed and what charge treatment applies to attendance, labour or transport. Do not assume that a possible warranty concern automatically uses the standard no-repair visiting charge or automatically results in a free visit.

If the findings establish a separate paid repair, obtain that scope and complete amount before approving it. A review request should not silently become permission for unrelated work. Keep the assessment result and any new approval attached to the original history.

Three returning faults with different questions

Illustrative situations: These examples explain repair decisions. They are not records of actual FixMyWasher customer jobs.

No draining after a recent new pump

Suppose the earlier record identifies a new pump and the washer again retains water. Report the job for review of the supplied part and drainage system. Do not assume either a defective pump or a new blockage without assessment, and do not immediately order another pump from the symptom alone.

A programme interruption never clearly resolved

Suppose an original PCB was repaired but only a short check was possible, and the longer programme still stops. Review the initial complaint, repair scope and testing limitation. The next step should establish the cause and applicable repair terms, not rely only on the display having lit up at handover.

A different fault after normal use

Suppose a door seal was replaced and the machine later develops an unrelated-looking display complaint. The timing deserves a factual report, but does not itself prove a connection. Ask for assessment before treating the new concern as either covered by the seal warranty or automatically outside every earlier responsibility.

Clarify charges before another attendance

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Share your Bangalore locality or PIN code and confirm all applicable charges together before booking. ₹200 is not an advertised complete repair or parts-replacement price.

Ask how the visiting and inspection charges are treated if a repair proceeds. No automatic waiver, deduction or additional charge should be assumed. Your approved quote should make clear whether these amounts are included, credited or payable separately, and state any applicable tax and the final payable total.

For a recurrence, provide the earlier job reference before agreeing a new standard booking. Ask specifically which review, warranty or service arrangement applies. The confirmed no-repair term does not by itself settle every follow-up situation.

If another diagnostic stage is proposed, ask what it will establish, what it costs and how the amount is treated if further work proceeds. Keep any actual credit or waiver explicit. Do not assume that previous expenditure will always be deducted or can never be considered.

When another assessment may help

A second opinion may be useful when the cause remains unexplained, the proposal has become substantial or the original provider cannot complete a defined review. Share the earlier work and findings so the new assessment does not begin with an incomplete history.

Before another business dismantles the machine, review any unresolved manufacturer or repair claim and its applicable terms. A second opinion should have its own scope, cost and record. Do not conceal earlier repairs or ask a provider to endorse a diagnosis it has not assessed.

Compare explanations and supported options, not just the number of parts on each quote. If two proposals differ, ask what finding or model-specific service arrangement explains the difference. More expensive work is not automatically more thorough, and a smaller quote is not automatically complete.

Avoid approving repeated work without a complete plan

A modest second step can be sensible if it has a clear purpose. The concern is open-ended spending without understanding what each step establishes or restores. Ask for an updated plan when multiple attempts or confirmed faults are involved.

As indicative FixMyWasher guide estimates, an existing PCB repair with normal refitting is ₹1,800–₹4,500 and a new compatible PCB with normal fitting is ₹3,500–₹12,000, with some models higher. These are different options, not a compulsory sequence or a guaranteed follow-up price.

Brand, exact model, part, scope, access and location affect the actual quote. Confirm separately applicable inspection, transport, access and tax treatment. Do not add every earlier option to a new proposal without an agreement explaining work already authorised and the amount still payable.

Reconsider the whole repair route when the scope changes

If repeated assessment establishes several essential faults or a much larger assembly requirement, step back and compare the complete restoration cost with a suitable replacement. Use known findings rather than counting hypothetical future failures as certain bills.

Money already spent is relevant to history, but it is not recovered merely by continuing to spend. Review applicable earlier support first, then judge the additional decision from today onward. The repair-or-replace guide helps organise that comparison.

A recurrence alone does not mean the washer must be discarded. A correctable problem with earlier work or a separate modest fault can lead to a different decision from major combined damage. Let the supported findings determine the next step.

Keep the supplied-part promise and other terms distinct

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Read the repair warranty guide for the difference between supplied parts, repaired originals and attendance arrangements. This page does not decide a claim before the current fault has been assessed.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do first if the same problem returns after repair?

Stop unsafe operation and contact the original provider with the job reference and symptom. Request review of the present cause and its relationship to the earlier work.

Does a returning symptom prove the repair was wrong?

Not automatically. It requires assessment. The supplied part, earlier work, an unresolved condition or another cause may be relevant, and the findings should explain the next proposal.

Should I pay immediately for another identical part?

Ask what new findings support replacement and how the previous work and terms are being reviewed before approving another part.

What if the machine works when the technician arrives?

Describe the earlier pattern and provide existing records. Ask what was checked, what remains uncertain and what defined next step is proposed.

Is every follow-up visit free?

Do not assume that. Confirm the actual warranty or service attendance arrangements for the reported concern before booking.

Does FixMyWasher cover the whole washer for a year after one repair?

No. Its confirmed one-year warranty applies to new parts it supplies. Other components and service terms need their own scope and confirmation.

Can I ask another technician for an opinion?

Yes, but clarify the new assessment scope and consider any unresolved claim terms before additional dismantling. Share the full earlier repair history.

Should I run the washer repeatedly to record the fault?

Do not recreate hazardous conditions. Use observations or evidence already available and stop for electrical warnings, serious leakage or severe mechanical contact.

When should I reconsider repairing the whole machine?

When supported findings materially change the total necessary work or several essential faults remain. Compare complete costs and usefulness, rather than deciding from recurrence alone.

What information should the recurrence report include?

The job reference, model, earlier work, when the symptom returned, behaviour observed, existing messages or photos and any safety or access concern.
