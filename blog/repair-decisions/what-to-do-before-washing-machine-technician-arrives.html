What to do before the washing machine technician arrives

Have the model, symptom, earlier repair records and booking details ready, and make the surrounding area safely accessible. Tell the provider about trapped water or laundry, restricted installation and any electrical warning. You do not need to dismantle, move or repeatedly run the washer to prepare for an appointment.

The aim is to give the technician useful information and a workable visit, while keeping diagnosis and repair approval clear. This checklist is for an ordinary Bangalore repair enquiry; active fire, electric shock risk or another immediate danger needs appropriate urgent help rather than waiting for the scheduled visit.

If an item cannot be prepared safely, tell the booking team. A useful advance explanation is better than creating another problem while trying to make the visit convenient.

Deal with a safety warning before routine preparation

Stop using the washer for smoke, sparks, burning smells, electric shock, repeated protective-device trips, serious leakage near electricity or severe mechanical contact. Keep children, pets and other people away from the affected area.

Isolate the supply only if it is safely accessible from a dry position. Do not touch wet plugs, damaged leads or exposed wiring, and do not enter standing water to reach a switch. Get competent help where isolation or access is unsafe. For active fire or immediate danger, contact emergency services.

Tell the provider what happened and whether the machine has been isolated. Do not switch it back on to demonstrate the event when the technician arrives unless the technician has assessed the situation and is managing appropriate safe checks. Preparation should preserve information without recreating a hazard.

Find the full model without unnecessary moving

Look for an accessible rating label or use purchase documents and earlier service records. Send a readable photograph if available. A brand name and capacity alone may not identify the exact machine or component arrangement.

If the label is behind a stacked, built-in or heavy machine, do not pull it out just to obtain the number. State that access is restricted and provide the information already available. The provider can plan suitable identification and handling.

Keep the serial or production details if the provider requests them for the job, but avoid sending unrelated personal documents unnecessarily. An image of the relevant appliance label is usually more useful than a wide photograph of private household paperwork.

Prepare a short description of what actually happens

Use ordinary language: whether the machine starts, where the programme stops, whether water remains, what message appears and when the change began. If you do not know one of those details, say so rather than running another cycle just to fill in the description.

Mention whether the issue happens on every normal use or intermittently. Include a recent move, new installation, supply change or earlier repair if relevant. Those events are context, not proof of the cause.

Do not purchase a motor, pump or PCB because an online symptom list suggests it. The provider should establish the fault before recommending a major component. A clear complaint allows that assessment to remain open to the actual findings.

Tell the technician about trapped water and laundry

If the machine opens normally and the manual permits it under safe conditions, you can remove accessible laundry. If the door remains locked, water is retained or the temperature is uncertain, do not force access. Inform the provider instead.

Do not open a drain filter or loosen a hose without the appropriate model instructions and safe conditions. Water quantity, temperature and electrical surroundings matter. A generic instruction to “empty the washer before we arrive” should be clarified if it is not safely possible.

Let the provider know if clothes are urgently needed so the appointment can be planned, but do not treat urgency as a reason to bypass a lock. The technician can assess the appropriate handling as part of the agreed scope.

Clear the surroundings without moving the appliance yourself

Remove light, easily handled items from the immediate work area where safe. Keep the route free of loose clutter and provide reasonable lighting. Move pets and children away from the work area so the technician can assess the machine without avoidable interruptions.

Do not unstack a dryer, lift the washer, remove a countertop or pull the appliance out through tight connections as routine preparation. Those tasks can change the access plan and may need appropriate people or equipment. Describe them before the appointment.

If the washer is in a balcony, utility cupboard or another restricted area, explain the actual access and surrounding conditions. A photograph taken safely from outside the area can help. Do not promise that ordinary attendance includes unplanned building work or heavy handling.

Explain supply conditions rather than changing them

Tell the provider if electricity or water is unavailable, the machine is already disconnected or a building connection is suspected. Those conditions can affect what checks are possible. An appliance visit may not include correction of the building’s plumbing or electrical installation.

Do not arrange improvised extension leads, bypass protective devices or make temporary wiring changes so the technician can test the washer. If an electrician or plumber is needed, have the appropriate scope discussed.

If a routine water shutdown or building restriction is known in advance, notify the provider. A visit can still have a useful assessment purpose, but a water-dependent completion check should not be assumed available when the supply is off.

Keep purchase, repair and warranty information together

Have the purchase invoice if available, manufacturer or extended-plan details and recent repair records ready. A photo or digital copy can be sufficient for an initial discussion, subject to the actual provider’s requirements.

If manufacturer cover may apply, establish the official claim route before commissioning third-party dismantling or repair. If an earlier paid repair has a possible warranty concern, provide its reference before booking the issue as an entirely new job.

State what was actually done previously and what was only recommended. A record of a new supplied pump is different from a cleaning visit or an unaccepted pump quote. Accurate history makes the next assessment more useful and avoids assumptions about parts that were never changed.

Three visits that benefit from advance preparation

Illustrative situations: These examples explain repair decisions. They are not records of actual FixMyWasher customer jobs.

A washer installed under a counter

Suppose the machine sits tightly below a fixed counter and its rear connections cannot be accessed safely by the owner. A model record and safe overview photo help the provider plan. Pulling the washer out without an access plan could create another problem and is not required preparation.

Water and laundry remain behind a locked door

Suppose the washer stopped mid-cycle and the door will not release. Tell the provider about both conditions. Do not force the handle or open a drain component with uncertain water temperature. The appointment should account for safe handling before testing or repair.

The same fault after a recent repair

Suppose a newly supplied part was fitted recently and the original complaint returned. Send that job record and ask how the recurrence will be reviewed. Booking an unexplained new replacement before the earlier scope is considered can lead to a confused diagnosis and bill.

Arrange who can approve the diagnosis and repair

Make sure someone can describe the complaint and receive the findings, or arrange a reachable decision-maker. A building caretaker or family member allowing access should not automatically be assumed to approve an unlimited repair budget.

Before the visit, agree what assessment is authorised. After the finding, ask for the proposed work and complete amount. If the repair requires further dismantling or a part order, approve that defined stage rather than an open-ended instruction to do whatever is needed.

If the person paying cannot attend, keep the approval in a clear message tied to the quotation. Include material scope changes later. This helps the technician proceed with a concrete decision and leaves a useful record for handover.

Confirm the appointment and the provider’s identity

Keep the booking reference, agreed window and contact route. If a manufacturer-appointed provider is expected, retain the official service reference and confirmation. If the arriving business differs from the booking, check through the original trusted channel before work begins.

Share reasonable property access information such as a building entry process or parking restriction that affects attendance. Do not distribute unnecessary security details or access credentials beyond what is needed for the visit.

If the appointment window changes, ask for an update rather than assuming a guaranteed completion time. Attendance, diagnosis and finished repair are different stages. The repair-time guide explains how parts and checks affect the schedule.

Confirm the booking charges before attendance

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Share your Bangalore locality or PIN code and confirm all applicable charges together before booking. ₹200 is not an advertised complete repair or parts-replacement price.

Ask how the visiting and inspection charges are treated if a repair proceeds. No automatic waiver, deduction or additional charge should be assumed. Your approved quote should make clear whether these amounts are included, credited or payable separately, and state any applicable tax and the final payable total.

Ask what assessment is covered and whether restricted access, off-site work or another stage would need a separate agreement. The booking confirmation should make the no-repair outcome understandable.

Do not interpret ₹200 as the price of a pump, board or complete repair. A specific part or corrective job requires an approved quote after the relevant findings. Brand, model, part, scope, access and location affect that proposal.

Leave room for the findings and completion discussion

At the visit, give the technician the symptom history and allow appropriate assessment within the agreed scope. Ask for an explanation of the findings before additional repair. You do not need to stand beside exposed parts or assist with internal work.

If repair proceeds, ask what relevant checks will be completed and what paperwork you will receive. Where the job cannot finish that day, clarify the next stage, item handover if any, current safe-use status and expected update.

Keep a few minutes for the final explanation when practical. Use the post-repair checklist to review the supplied part, checks, total and actual terms. Preparation is most useful when it also leads to an understandable handover.

Understand the terms before new parts are supplied

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

If the appointment concerns an earlier supplied part, identify that job when booking. If the proposal repairs an original component instead, ask for its separate terms before approving the option.

Prepare your FixMyWasher appointment

Send the locality or PIN code, accessible model details, short symptom description and relevant repair history. Mention potential cover, retained water and any access or safety concern. Confirm the assessment arrangements and complete applicable booking charges.

Then keep the surroundings safely accessible and the decision-maker reachable. Leave moving, dismantling and internal testing to the agreed professional plan, and ask for a clear quote before corrective work proceeds.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pull the washing machine out before the technician arrives?

No routine heavy moving is required. Describe restricted, built-in or stacked access and agree suitable handling arrangements. Clear only safe, light clutter around the work area.

What if I cannot reach the model label?

Use available purchase or earlier service records and tell the provider the label is inaccessible. Do not move a heavy machine unsafely just to obtain it.

Should I run the washer to demonstrate the fault before the visit?

Use observations already available. Do not recreate electrical warnings, serious leaks or severe mechanical contact. The technician should manage appropriate checks after assessment.

Must I remove trapped laundry?

Remove it only if normal access is available and safe under the model’s instructions. Do not force a locked door; tell the provider about retained water or laundry.

Should I drain the machine myself?

Only follow an owner-approved model procedure when conditions are safe and clear. Do not open a filter or hose with uncertain water quantity, temperature or electrical risk; request help instead.

What documents should be ready?

Prepare the model, booking reference, purchase or warranty information if relevant, and recent repair records with supplied-part descriptions.

What if the building has no water during the appointment?

Tell the provider in advance. Some assessment may be possible, but water-dependent checks should be recorded as unavailable rather than assumed complete.

Can a family member approve the repair for me?

Agree who is authorised and reachable to review the defined scope and price. Permission to enter the property should not be treated as unlimited repair approval.

What do I confirm about charges?

Confirm the attendance and inspection scope, the complete no-repair amount and how booking charges are treated if repair proceeds. Additional work needs its own clear approval.

How do I prepare for a recurrence under an earlier repair?

Send the earlier job record and current symptom before arranging a new paid repair. Ask how the review and attendance will be handled under the actual terms.
