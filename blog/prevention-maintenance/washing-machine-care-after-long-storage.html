Washing machine care after long storage: inspect the history before pressing Start

Review where and how the washer was stored, inspect safely accessible condition, and follow the exact model’s return-to-use or installation procedure before loading clothes. A machine that stayed protected in place has a different starting point from one that was moved, disconnected, exposed to water or left with an unresolved fault.

Do not begin with a universal hot wash, descaler or reset sequence. The first decision is whether normal owner preparation is appropriate or whether the appliance, connections or electrical installation need assessment before power is restored.

Do not open panels, reach into hidden spaces or scrape damage to improve the inspection. Existing photographs and a clear history can support an enquiry without creating another risk.

Six stages before ordinary laundry resumes

  1. Reconstruct the storage history

    Note the approximate duration, location, whether the washer was moved and what preparation was completed. Ask the previous user about water exposure, faults and any product left inside. Record uncertainty instead of guessing that everything was drained or isolated.

  2. Review the room and accessible condition

    From a safe dry position, look for visible damage, unexpected wetness, damaged connections or signs of exposure. Keep clear of wet electrical areas. Do not assume a clean cabinet means concealed components were unaffected.

  3. Confirm the correct installation route

    If the machine moved, use its location, support, connection and transport-restraint instructions. If it stayed in place, review any household changes around it. Do not remove unidentified bolts or add improvised adapters to make the installation fit.

  4. Restore supplies only under the applicable procedure

    Follow the model’s reconnection and return-to-use instructions when the condition is suitable. Water isolation and fittings must be reliable. If electrical safety or connection compatibility is uncertain, obtain appropriate help before proceeding.

  5. Complete the specified preparation and check

    Use the exact model’s prescribed return or maintenance procedure, including any product removal. There is no universal hottest programme, detergent amount or descaling dose. Stop if a new leak, severe movement, trip or other unsafe condition appears.

  6. Record the result before normal use

    Note what was completed, whether the relevant operation and connections were checked and any remaining limitation. If a symptom remains, move to diagnosis rather than repeatedly running empty cycles. Resume suitable laundry only after the applicable preparation is complete.

Time unused is useful context, not a diagnosis

Two washers unused for the same period can have very different conditions. One may have remained protected and undisturbed; another may have been moved, stored in a damp area or left with a leak.

Record whether it was disconnected, whether hoses were replaced and whether any transport restraints were fitted. These details help identify what must be restored or commissioned without assuming a part has failed.

If you inherited or bought the appliance, ask for its model, prior fault history and any service record. Do not treat a seller’s statement that it worked before storage as a complete present-condition assessment.

A long storage period does not automatically justify a motor, pump, bearing or board replacement. The proposed work should be connected to an actual finding in the present machine.

What a safe external review can establish

Look at visible hoses, plugs, accessible surfaces and the surrounding floor without dismantling anything. A cut hose, damaged cable, displaced seal or unexplained wet area is enough to stop the routine and seek help.

Do not test a damaged cable by plugging it in, or a suspect hose by restoring supply to see whether it bursts or leaks. A visual defect deserves the appropriate repair decision before operation.

An inaccessible section remains uninspected. Record that limitation instead of describing the entire appliance as checked. A professional visit may be needed for safe access or a specific concern.

For exterior marks or coating changes, see the exterior-condition guide. Do not disguise corrosion with paint or polish before its significance is assessed.

If the washer was moved, commissioning comes before cleaning

Confirm the installation matches the exact manual: suitable location and support, required clearances, correct supply and waste arrangements, and appropriate handling of transport parts.

A front-loader may use model-specific shipping restraints; other designs differ. Remove or install only the parts identified by the manual and the correct procedure. Do not loosen every rear fastener because another machine has transport bolts.

Check the final position rather than only the machine while it stands forward. A hose or cord can be trapped during placement, and a newly fitted stand or floor position can change support.

Arrange help for a stacked, heavy or tightly enclosed appliance. Installation work should not require pulling on the door, drawer or hoses or holding an unstable machine while another person reaches beneath it.

Why “run the hottest wash” is not a complete restart method

The correct procedure depends on what the manufacturer specifies and what preparation was performed. It may include installation checks, a particular cleaning method or removal of a storage product.

The Whirlpool return guidance cited below refers back to installation and gives a product-family procedure. Its named programmes, regional water connections and winterisation details are not universal instructions for every washer.

Do not add descaler, bleach or detergent simply because the machine smells stale or has been unused. Identify the appropriate task and permitted product first, and never mix cleaners.

If the model gives no clear procedure for your situation, ask manufacturer support using the full model and storage history. An uncertain method should be clarified before it becomes an experimental sequence of empty washes.

Treat a musty smell as a specific observation

Describe where the smell is noticeable: the empty drum, accessible drawer, door area, room or first laundry load. Note whether there is retained water or another operating symptom.

An odour may justify appropriate owner cleaning when the machine is otherwise suitable to use, but it does not override a water-exposure concern, damaged wiring or an unresolved leak.

Use the cleaning guide to choose the relevant task, and the smell-prevention guide for the routine after normal operation resumes.

If the smell returns promptly after the correct task, record what was completed and request assessment rather than using increasing chemical strength. A changed fragrance is not evidence that the underlying condition was resolved.

Water exposure needs a different return decision

If rain or floodwater may have entered the appliance, do not use a waiting period or a dry exterior as proof that it can be restarted. Keep clear of unsafe wet areas and arrange the appropriate assessment.

The household electrical installation and the appliance may need separate review. A repair quotation for one component should not imply that affected room wiring has also been assessed.

Do not use an extension, another socket or repeated breaker resets to bypass a problem that appears on return. Report the exposure history and exact symptom.

Read the exposure-response guide for the distinction between ordinary room care and a potentially water-damaged appliance. Storage time does not cancel that distinction.

Interpret the first permitted check accurately

If the prescribed check completes normally, record what was checked and the applicable preparation. Do not describe a brief empty run as proof that every possible load and internal component has been tested.

If filling, draining, closure or movement is abnormal, stop the routine at the appropriate point and report the specific result. A failed preparation check should guide diagnosis rather than automatically trigger another cleaner.

If the supply was unavailable or a required check could not be completed, keep that limitation visible to the next user. The machine should not be presented as fully ready simply because the visit ended.

For a professional return-to-use visit, ask whether the scope includes installation, cleaning, fault diagnosis or a combination. Each should have an explicit purpose and a relevant completion record.

Warranty when a new part is supplied

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Routine cleaning without a new supplied part does not create a one-year warranty on the whole appliance. Confirm the agreed cleaning scope and any service or revisit terms separately.

Frequently asked questions

Should I start with the hottest wash after storage?

Not as a universal rule. Review condition and follow the exact model’s return-to-use and installation instructions first.

Does being unused for a year mean parts must be replaced?

No. Duration is context, not a diagnosis. Replacement needs relevant present-condition findings.

Can I trust a machine that worked before storage?

That is useful history, but current condition, exposure and installation still need review.

What if the washer was moved?

Complete the applicable installation and commissioning procedure, including model-specific transport and connection requirements.

Can I remove all rear bolts before restarting?

No. Identify only the transport restraints specified for the exact model; other fasteners serve different purposes.

What if a storage chemical was used?

Identify it and follow the exact model-approved removal procedure before laundry use. Do not improvise a neutralising mixture.

Can a dry-looking flooded washer be tested?

Do not energise it as a test. Appropriate assessment is needed before return to use.

Does a successful empty run prove everything is fine?

It establishes the conditions actually checked, not every loaded operation or hidden component.

What if a musty smell returns after cleaning?

Record the task, product and timing and seek assessment of the remaining complaint rather than escalating chemicals.

What should a restart service include?

A clearly defined combination of inspection, installation, cleaning or diagnosis, with the relevant checks and limitations recorded.
