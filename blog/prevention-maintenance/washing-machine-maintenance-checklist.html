Washing machine maintenance checklist: record what is done, due or unresolved

Use this checklist to organise the tasks that apply to your exact washer—not to perform every task today. Start with the model manual, mark each relevant item and keep faults separate from routine care. A task is complete only when its required restoration and checks are finished.

The status choices below work in your browser without sending a form. They may reset when you reload or leave the page, so keep a separate note or use your browser’s print or save-to-PDF option if you want a record. This is an owner-maintenance aid, not an inspection certificate or a guarantee against future faults.

Stop conditions come before checklist completion

Do not continue routine care through these conditions
ObservationPriority
Water near electricity, a shock sensation, repeated trip or burning smellStop use, keep clear of unsafe areas and obtain appropriate qualified help.
Possible rain or floodwater entryDo not restart as a test; arrange suitable assessment.
Severe movement, striking nearby surfaces or strained connectionsStop operation safely and request assessment before further use.
Hot or uncontrolled retained waterUse the exact safe recovery route; do not open a filter or force a door.
Damaged hose, torn seal, loose assembly or active leakArrange assessment rather than marking the relevant care task complete.
An unknown or mixed cleaning product has been usedIdentify it and obtain suitable guidance before adding another chemical.

If one of these applies, record the condition as needing attention. You do not need to finish the rest of the checklist before requesting help. Do not recreate a fault to improve the record.

Set up the record in six steps

  1. Identify the exact appliance

    Record the full model, front- or top-load design, any washer-dryer function and the correct manual. Use a safely accessible label or purchase document; do not move or dismantle the appliance just to fill a field.

  2. Select the applicable tasks

    Read the groups below and identify the components your model actually has. A drain filter, lint collector or automatic-dosing reservoir may not be present or owner-serviceable. Mark an item not applicable only when its scope genuinely does not fit.

  3. Enter the documented interval or trigger

    Use the manual’s calendar interval, wash count, reminder or other condition. Keep each task separate. Do not invent one monthly rule for the drum, every filter and all hoses.

  4. Complete only due and manageable owner work

    Open the linked procedure for detail and follow the exact model. Allow time for isolation, cooling, retained-water handling and restoration where needed. A checkbox is not a substitute for the full safe procedure.

  5. Mark the outcome honestly

    Use Done only for a completed applicable check or task. Use Needs attention for an unresolved condition and Not checked where the task was not reviewed. Do not use Not applicable to conceal missing access or uncertainty.

  6. Keep the next action with the record

    Note what remains, who will address it and the next due trigger. Preserve the completed record outside this page if needed. After service, update the finding and result rather than only writing that someone visited.

Group A: normal-use observations

Load and closing area

Confirm the load is suitable for the selected programme and fabric instructions, with loose objects removed and laundry clear of the closing area. This is an observation before use, not permission to force the door or fill every programme to the headline capacity.

Detergent and dispensing

Confirm the current detergent type, dose and placement are understood. Include any automatic-dosing requirements. If an unknown cleaner was added or product compatibility is uncertain, mark Needs attention instead of starting another cycle to clear it.

Visible change in operation

Review any new leak, severe movement, repeated error, smell or other concern from ordinary use. No special test cycle is required for this checklist. A known unresolved symptom belongs in the open-issues record even if the machine sometimes completes a wash.

End-of-use care and household handover

Complete the model’s permitted accessible wiping and drying where appropriate, and tell the next user about an unresolved issue. Door or drawer ventilation must remain safe around children, pets and the room layout. Do not leave an interrupted maintenance task unmarked.

For the full operating routine, use daily-use care. The purpose of this group is a brief, repeatable review, not daily disassembly.

Group B: manual-scheduled owner tasks

Drum or tub maintenance programme

Record the exact programme and due trigger. Follow its empty-machine and additive instructions. A cleaning reminder is not proof of mineral scale, and completion does not diagnose every operating fault. See drum clean versus descaling.

Drawer or automatic-dosing care

Use the exact system procedure and restore all permitted removable inserts. A conventional drawer and an automatic-dosing assembly are different tasks. See drawer cleaning; do not soak an unfamiliar electronic assembly.

Accessible door-rubber care

Where applicable, clean only the permitted surfaces without detaching the seal or ring. Record cuts, displacement or persistent leakage separately. Use door-rubber care; a stain alone does not establish a need for replacement.

Owner-accessible drain-filter care

Confirm the actual access arrangement and use the full isolation, cooling and controlled-drain procedure before removal. Restore and verify the assembly. See drain-filter cleaning. If safe access is unavailable, mark Needs attention, not Done.

Top-load lint collector, if fitted

Follow the model’s removal, cleaning and restoration method only for an owner-serviceable collector. It is not the same as an inlet screen or pump filter. See top-load lint-filter care.

Inlet screen and any permitted descaling

Treat these as separate tasks in your personal schedule. Use inlet-screen guidance and hard-water planning as relevant. Neither a TDS number nor the city name supplies a universal chemical dose.

Use the cleaning-frequency guide to record the trigger for each applicable task. This group lists the scope; it does not supply one mandatory interval.

Group C: accessible installation and environmental review

Visible hose condition and routing

From safe access, look for damage, leakage, crushing or strain without loosening connections. Record replacement dates where known and use the actual model’s guidance. See hose maintenance.

Support, clearance and movement history

Note whether the appliance was moved, an accessory was fitted or severe movement appeared. Use vibration prevention for the decision route. Do not perform an unsafe spin test or add improvised supports to finish this row.

Exterior and recurring wetness

Review accessible spills, coating changes and sources of repeated wetness without scraping or removing panels. See exterior and rust prevention. Cleaning a surface does not assess hidden corrosion or structural condition.

Rain exposure and electrical-area concerns

Record known rain entry, flooding or unsafe wet electrical areas. Use monsoon care for the stop-and-assess route. A dry-looking surface or a completed checklist is not clearance to restart an affected appliance.

This group records owner observations. It does not ask you to test earthing, open sockets, inspect live wiring or certify the household electrical installation.

Group D: after maintenance, moving or service

Restoration is complete

Confirm all owner-removed items and intended connections were restored under the procedure. If a filter cap, hose fitting or seal position is uncertain, keep the machine out of ordinary use and mark Needs attention.

Relevant completion check is recorded

Record what was actually checked after the work and under what conditions. A supply unavailable during the visit or an intermittent fault not reproduced is a limitation to retain, not a reason to mark the entire appliance verified.

Invoice and supplied-part details are retained

Keep the findings, approved work, actual supplied part if any and applicable warranty or service terms. Distinguish cleaning from repair and appliance work from household plumbing or electrical work.

Next user and next task are clear

Communicate whether normal use can resume under the completed procedure or whether a professional decision remains. Record the next maintenance trigger and who will follow up any open issue.

Use Not applicable for this group when no relevant work or movement has occurred. If work occurred but the result is uncertain, use Needs attention and describe the uncertainty.

What belongs to a professional scope?

Cabinet removal, internal component diagnosis, electrical testing, inaccessible hose work and structural concerns do not become owner tasks because they appear on a maintenance checklist. Describe the finding and request the appropriate assessment.

Ask a provider to distinguish a general service from diagnosis of an active complaint. A cleaning package may be useful for a defined need, but it should not be sold as proof that every component was tested.

Before approving parts, request the supporting finding, compatibility and full quotation. A Needs attention status identifies an unresolved question; it does not authorise a pump, board, seal or suspension replacement.

Check any relevant manufacturer coverage before independent work. FixMyWasher does not claim manufacturer-authorised status, and its supplied-part warranty is not a blanket warranty on the machine.

Keep a record without assuming this page saves it

Use the browser’s print command if you want a paper copy or PDF of the current checklist. Review the print preview and resulting file to confirm your selected controls are represented by your browser.

The choices are local form controls with no submission or saving script. They may reset on reload or navigation, and they are not attached automatically to a call or WhatsApp enquiry. Keep important findings in your own note as well.

When printing a blank checklist, leave all tasks Not checked and write the relevant dates and triggers in your own record. Reuse it as a review aid, not as a reason to perform every listed procedure at once.

For a service discussion, share only the relevant task, observation and history. The provider does not need a claim that all boxes were completed; an accurate account of the unresolved issue is more useful.

Bangalore visiting and inspection charges

The visiting charge is ₹200 if no repair is carried out. Inspection charges depend on location. Share your Bangalore locality or PIN code and confirm all applicable charges together before booking. ₹200 is not an advertised complete repair or parts-replacement price.

Ask how the visiting and inspection charges are treated if a repair proceeds. No automatic waiver, deduction or additional charge should be assumed. Your approved quote should make clear whether these amounts are included, credited or payable separately, and state any applicable tax and the final payable total.

Warranty when a new part is supplied

New parts supplied by FixMyWasher carry a one-year warranty. Ask for the supplied part to be identified on your job record or invoice. This applies to that new part; it does not turn the job into a one-year warranty on the entire washing machine.

Repairing an existing component is different from supplying a new one. Confirm the terms for repaired boards, retained parts, workmanship, labour, revisits and customer-supplied items separately. Do not infer those terms from the new-parts warranty.

Keep the quote, approved changes and completion record together. If the symptom returns, report what happened and when rather than assuming that the same part has failed. Stop using the washer if it leaks significantly, trips the circuit or develops a burning smell.

Routine cleaning without a new supplied part does not create a one-year warranty on the whole appliance. Confirm the agreed cleaning scope and any service or revisit terms separately.

Frequently asked questions

Should I perform every listed task today?

No. Select applicable tasks and follow their documented triggers. The list is an organiser, not one mandatory full-service procedure.

Does Done mean the entire washer is safe?

No. It records the stated observation or completed task and its required finishing steps. It is not an electrical or structural certification.

When should I use Not applicable?

Only when the component or task genuinely does not fit the model or situation. Uncertainty or difficult access belongs in Needs attention.

What if a task is not due yet?

Keep its next trigger in your schedule and do not mark the task completed merely because it can wait.

Are my choices sent to FixMyWasher?

No. These local browser controls have no submission or saving script. Describe the relevant item directly in a booking enquiry.

Will the checklist remember my choices?

Do not rely on that. Choices may reset on reload or navigation; keep a separate note or check your browser’s printed output.

Can I use this as a technician inspection certificate?

No. It is an owner-maintenance record. Professional findings and any safety assessment require their own appropriate documentation.

Does Needs attention mean I need a new part?

No. It identifies an unresolved observation, incomplete task or uncertainty that needs the appropriate next step.

Which cleaning frequency should I enter?

Use the exact model’s calendar, count or reminder rule for each task. Do not impose one interval on every component.

What should I keep after professional work?

The finding, approved scope, supplied-part details if any, relevant checks and limitations, plus applicable warranty and service terms.
