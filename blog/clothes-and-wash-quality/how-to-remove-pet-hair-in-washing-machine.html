How to Remove Pet Hair from Laundry and the Washer

Reduce loose pet hair on the fabric before loading a standard washer, then follow the textile and machine instructions. Repeatedly washing a heavily hairy item without reviewing preparation can leave the source of the problem unchanged.

This guide separates hair remaining on clothes, visible hair in accessible washer areas and an actual operating fault. It does not assume your machine has a dedicated pet-hair filter or programme.

Start with the result on the laundry

Start outside the appliance with a fabric-safe lint tool used as directed. Confirm that the garment or removable pet-bedding cover is machine washable and fits the selected programme. Afterward, review only the owner-maintenance areas identified in your model manual.

If the washer stops draining, leaks or displays a recurring message, report that separately. Hair on laundry does not by itself prove a failed pump, and no routine wash guarantees complete hair or allergen removal.

Remove what you can safely before the wash

Use a lint roller or another fabric-compatible hair-removal tool according to its instructions. Maytag’s pet-hair guidance recommends reducing loose hair before a standard wash. Be gentle with delicate surfaces and stop if the tool pulls fibres or damages the finish.

Collect removed hair for appropriate disposal rather than washing clumps down a sink or pushing them into the washer outlet. This preparation is a practical way to reduce what enters the appliance; it is not a promise that every embedded hair will be removed. Avoid scraping valuable textiles with improvised hard edges.

Check pet bedding as an item with its own construction

A removable cover, filled bed, foam insert and waterproof liner are not the same load. Read each item’s care label and the washer’s restrictions. A cover may be washable even when the complete bed is unsuitable for the drum.

Remove loose debris using an appropriate method before loading. Do not assume a large machine rating makes every pet bed acceptable, or force an item into place because it is compressible while dry. If the material or construction is excluded, use an appropriate cleaning route instead of trying a stronger cycle or extra detergent.

Load for the selected programme and fabric

Use the permitted quantity and preparation for the textile. Do not mix a heavily shedding or hairy item with unrelated delicate clothes simply to complete a full load. A practical comparison records which items were washed together and how much loose hair was removed first.

If one fabric holds hair while other suitable loads are satisfactory, that pattern does not identify a failed component. If the machine also fails to rinse or drain, include the operating change. Keep these observations distinct so the service enquiry addresses an actual function rather than assuming every remaining hair is a technical fault.

Use pet-specific features only if the model has them

Some machines include a dedicated pet-hair option or filter. Maytag documents such features on selected models, with particular operation and maintenance instructions. Their existence does not mean a standard washer has a hidden equivalent or should accept an aftermarket insert.

Identify the full model and follow its manual for the fitted feature. Do not remove an agitator, open panels or copy a filter procedure from another design. A marketing comparison for one appliance is not a performance guarantee for yours, and a pet setting does not override fabric or bulky-item restrictions.

Clean accessible areas without pushing hair deeper

After safe unloading and isolation as instructed, follow the manual for wiping accessible areas or cleaning an owner-removable collector. Dispose of collected material appropriately. Do not probe drain paths, run the machine without a required filter or force a fitting that will not release.

For a drain-filter procedure, account for retained water and cooling before access. A lint collector and pump filter are different components and may require different handling. If the relevant area is inaccessible or the procedure is unclear, ask for model-specific help rather than turning a routine cleanup into dismantling.

Keep drying advice specific to the item and appliance

If a permitted dryer is used, follow its fabric settings, load allowance and lint-maintenance instructions. Maytag’s washer-and-dryer hair guide treats maintenance of the two appliances separately. Do not assume every garment can be put through a dryer first or that a washer-dryer has an ordinary removable dryer screen.

Record whether hair is visible after washing, after drying or at both stages. Do not use an unapproved dryer accessory, wipe or chemical as a universal collector. The relevant maintenance route depends on the actual appliance, and fabric damage is not an acceptable trade for removing a few remaining hairs.

Recognise when debris has become an operating enquiry

Tell the provider if water remains, drainage slows noticeably, a message recurs or leakage appears after the load. Include the hair and debris history, but do not diagnose a pump from that history alone. A restriction, installation concern and component failure need different findings.

Do not keep restarting a machine that cannot drain or pour a drain-opening chemical into it. Ask whether quoted work covers accessible cleaning, a confirmed restriction or an actual failed part. The provider should explain why any replacement is necessary and what water-removal and leak checks will follow.

Judge improvement without impossible guarantees

Compare suitable laundry with the same preparation, programme and product routine. Note whether the amount of visible hair improves and whether the appliance completes normally. Do not use a different fabric with a very different hair load as the only comparison.

Appliance repair does not certify textiles as allergen-free or resolve health concerns. A specialised care need should follow appropriate professional advice. For service follow-up, report the actual recurring function or visible debris and keep the invoice; a guarantee of zero hairs on every future garment would not be a meaningful parts warranty.

Keep checks safe for the appliance and the fabric

Use the exact appliance manual, garment care label and laundry-product instructions together. A programme or product suitable for one machine and fabric is not automatically suitable for another. Do not mix cleaning chemicals, add a stronger treatment to unidentified residue or use a maintenance cycle with clothes unless the manual explicitly permits it.

Carry out only owner-access maintenance, with the machine safely stopped and power isolated as instructed. Let hot water cool and account for retained water. Do not remove panels, force a fitting, bypass a safety device or open a locked door. Keep children and pets away from accessible drums and cleaning products.

Stop using the machine for burning smells, smoke, shocks, sparking, repeated electrical trips, violent movement or leakage near electrical fittings. Avoid touching wet plugs or switches and arrange safe isolation and appropriate help. Do not repeat an unsafe cycle to obtain a photograph.

What a useful wash-quality assessment should establish

An assessment should connect the laundry result with what the machine actually does. Share the full model, programme, load and products used, and say whether the cycle completes. The aim is to distinguish care or setup changes, relevant maintenance and a confirmed appliance fault.

A photograph helps describe a mark or result but does not identify its chemical composition or prove that a part is defective. Ask what was observed, what remains uncertain and why any proposed repair is expected to address the concern. An intermittent result may require a record of normal use rather than an immediate replacement.

Agree any additional dismantling, workshop work or further visit before it happens. Where the finding concerns the household water supply, drainage or a garment treatment, the appropriate plumber, supplier or fabric-care professional may be needed alongside or instead of appliance work.

What should justify a replacement part

Ask which component has actually been found faulty and how it explains the result. A photograph of the laundry result alone does not establish which appliance part, if any, needs replacement. A quotation should identify the exact work and the model-compatible part where replacement is necessary.

Confirm the source, condition, availability and total installed cost of a proposed component before approval. A manufacturer-original part, a compatible alternative and repair of an existing assembly are different offers. Any required configuration or additional visit should be included in the discussion.

Keep the finding and supplied-part description on the invoice. If the concern recurs, this record helps distinguish the same unresolved cause from another laundry, water-supply or appliance issue.

Retained water follows a heavily hairy load and recurs

Choose care advice, cleaning or repair from the finding

A change in laundry practice may be enough where the machine completes its functions and the result relates to a permitted load, programme or product choice. Maintenance should address an identified cleaning need. A persistent operating failure deserves a defined repair assessment.

Ask for necessary work to be separated from optional maintenance. A package called complete service should explain what is included and what result it is intended to improve. Repeating paid cleaning without reviewing a persistent fault may not resolve the concern.

For a major repair, compare the full amount, machine condition, known additional faults and parts availability. Appliance repair does not guarantee removal of an existing stain or restoration of damaged fabric. Discuss garment treatment separately using its care requirements.

How to judge whether the problem has improved

Verify the relevant drainage, filtering or other agreed function and compare suitable prepared laundry. Complete removal of every hair or allergen is not an appliance-repair guarantee.

Keep a short record of the programme, permitted load and product dose used for the comparison. Change only the relevant factor where a safe comparison is appropriate; changing several things together makes the result harder to interpret.

Ask what the technician demonstrated and what remains for normal observation. A short empty cycle does not prove that every laundry outcome has been corrected. Keep the invoice and report a recurrence with the stage, result and conditions. Do not recreate dangerous symptoms for a warranty enquiry.

Frequently asked questions

Should I remove hair before washing?

For a standard washer, reduce loose hair using a fabric-safe tool and confirm the item is washable.

Does every washer have a pet-hair filter?

No. Dedicated features and filter access are model-specific.

Can I wash an entire pet bed?

Only if both its construction and the appliance instructions permit it; a removable cover may have different care.

Will extra detergent remove every hair?

No. Use the correct dose and review preparation and loading.

Can I put a wet wipe in the drum to catch hair?

Do not use unapproved accessories or improvised collectors; follow the machine and product instructions.

Should I pour drain cleaner into a washer with hair buildup?

No. Use model-approved maintenance or request assessment of a drainage problem.

Can I clean any filter I find?

Only use the exact owner-access procedure for the identified component.

Does hair remaining prove the pump is faulty?

No. A pump quotation needs a relevant operating finding.

Can cleaning guarantee allergen-free laundry?

No. This guide and appliance service do not provide that certification.
